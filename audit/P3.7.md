## 4. Facts double-checked (CDC 2021 STI guideline in `scope/lectures/70351_23568845.md`, the course decks, current ACOG and USPSTF)
Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM (1 g at 150 kg or more); doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 7 days, azithromycin 1 g in pregnancy, test of cure about 4 weeks after treatment in pregnancy; DGI ceftriaxone 1 g IM or IV every 24 hours; PID outpatient regimen (ceftriaxone 500 mg IM, doxycycline and metronidazole for 14 days), admission criteria and the IUD note; chancroid regimens (azithromycin 1 g or ceftriaxone 250 mg); LGV doxycycline 21 days; donovanosis azithromycin at least 3 weeks; benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units (early) or three weekly doses (late latent), IV aqueous penicillin G 10 to 14 days for neurosyphilis; cervicitis signs and presumptive treatment; patient-collected vaginal swabs equal to clinician-collected swabs; wart treatments (podofilox contraindicated in pregnancy, sinecatechins not for HIV or immunosuppression, imiquimod and sinecatechins may weaken condoms, TCA and cryotherapy acceptable); condom efficacy 71 to 80 percent; PrEP onset (7 days anal, 21 days vaginal and injection) from the course deck; PEP 28 days within 72 hours; lenacapavir PrEP approved in 2025; HIV test windows and the CDC three-step algorithm; HSV suppression doses from the deck; HIV screening ages given as USPSTF 15 to 65 with the CDC range 13 to 64; hepatitis C 18 to 79 (USPSTF) and every pregnancy; GBS prophylaxis options (2020 CDC and ACOG); VZIG timing; MMR avoidance of conception for 4 weeks. Where the old draft said "beta-lactams fail" for chlamydia the wording is now "penicillins and cephalosporins are unreliable" because the CDC lists amoxicillin as a pregnancy alternative.
