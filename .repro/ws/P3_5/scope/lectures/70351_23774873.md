Perineum
The urogenital triangle is the anterior triangular region of the diamond-shaped perineum.
In anatomical position, it is oriented in a horizontal plane & contains the urethra and external genitalia.
The anal triangle is the posterior triangular region
In anatomical position, It is oriented in a plane that is almost vertical and contains the anal canal, anus,
external anal sphincter, inferior rectal nerve (branch of pudendal nerve), and the fat-filled ischiorectal fossa.

## Page 4

Urogenital Triangle: Perineal Membrane
Perineal membrane: thin fibromuscular horizontal layer with fibers running parallel to
pelvic diaphragm (i.e., levator ani), divides urogenital triangle into a deep (superior to
membrane) & superficial (inferior to membrane) pouch

Ischioanal (Ischiorectal) Fossa
•
Fossa filled with fat, poorly vascularized.
•
Allows for expansion of the anal canal during defecation & accommodates the fetus during childbirth.
•
Infections from one of the fossae can spread to the other & into the urogenital triangle.
• Contents: Fat, pudendal nerves and vessels giving the inferior rectal nerve & vessels

Female & Male Superficial Pouch
Male: Bulb & crura (attached) of the penis give rise to erectile sinuses of the penis – corpus
spongiosum & corpora cavernosa (free), respectively.
Female: Bulb of the vestibule & crura (attached) give rise to erectile sinuses of clitoris – glans clitoris
& corpora cavernosa (free) respectively.

## Page 12

Female & Male Superficial Pouch: Erectile Tissue
Root/attached components: sit/attach to the perineal membrane & are the bulb and crus.
Free components: corpora cavernosa - body of the clitoris (F) & body of the penis (M), and corpus
spongiosum –glans clitoris (F) and glans penis (M).

## Page 14

Superficial pouch contains external genitalia & associated skeletal muscles: bulbospongiosus,
ischiocavernosus, & superficial transverse perineal muscles. Because of their action during
urination/erection, these muscles are generally more developed in males.
• Sup. transverse perineal muscles: provide a firm base for the penis during erection
• Bulbospongiosus: aids in emptying of urine/semen.
• Ishiocavernosus: force blood from crura to corpora cavernosa, increasing turgidity of penis.

## Page 15

Superficial pouch contains external genitalia & associated skeletal muscles, including
bulbospongiosus, ischiocavernosus, & superficial transverse perineal muscles. Muscles cover
erectile tissue.
• Sup. transverse perineal muscle: support pelvic floor, fix perineal body
• Bulbospongiosus: “sphincter” of vagina aids in erection, compresses greater vestibular gland.
• Ishiocavernosus: forces blood from crura to corpora cavernosa, maintains erection of clitoris.

## Page 21

Autonomic Regulation of Sexual Function
Erection: parasympathetic (S2-4 via pelvic splanchnic nn)
•Emission: sympathetic (L1-2)
Smooth muscle contraction of the ductus deferens to move sperm into the urethra. Contractions of
seminal vesicles, prostate, and bulbourethral glands secrete seminal fluid into the urethra.
•Ejaculation:
•Sympathetic (L1-2): rhythmic contraction of smooth muscle in the ductus deferens & urethra; closure of
internal urethral sphincter. Somatic (S2-4, pudendal n): contraction of bulbospongiosus &
ischiocavernosus muscles compress the bases of the penile erectile tissue.
Point = Parasympathetic = Erection
Shoot = Sympathetic = Ejaculation

## Page 22

Pudendal Nerve Block
Pudendal nerve:
Passes out of the pelvis via the greater sciatic foramen &
around the sacrospinous ligament into the lesser sciatic
foramen, where it enters the pudendal (Alcock's)
canal; provides somatic innervation (S2-S4) to the skin &
skeletal muscles of the perineum & gives rise to inferior
rectal (anal), perineal, labial, & dorsal clitoral nerve
branches.
To relieve perineal pain during childbirth, pudendal nerve
block anesthesia may be performed by injecting a local
anesthetic agent into the tissues surrounding the pudendal
nerve.
To abolish sensation from the anterior part of the perineum,
an ilioinguinal nerve block is performed.

## Page 23

Male Urethral Rupture
Straddle injury: rupture of the spongy urethra below the perineal membrane. Urine extravasates to
the scrotum, penis, superficial perineum, and lower anterior abdominal wall.
Pelvic fractures: rupture of the membranous (intermediate part) of the urethra. Urine extravasates to
the deep perineal pouch.
