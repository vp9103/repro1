## Slide 6: Fertilization age Vs Gestational age

- The age of a developing conceptus can be measured either from the estimated day of fertilization (fertilization age) or from the day of the last normal menstrual period (Gestational age).
- Gestational age (LMP age) – Usual gestational age at birth is 40 weeks (38 weeks of development plus two weeks since LMP)
  - = 280 days
  - = 10 lunar months
- Fertilization age – Time since fertilization, usual age at birth is 38 weeks.
  - = 266 days
  - = 266/(365/12) = 8.87 ≈ 9 calendar months

## Slide 20

- Clinical correlation (Abnormal Implantation)
- Ectopic pregnancy (Extrauterine)
- Tubal pregnancy (most common)
- →Blastocyst implants within the ampulla.
- →Uterine bleeding, sudden onset of abdominal pain (appendicitis), Emergency, life-threatening, missed menstrual period, +ve hCG test.
- hCG → is a glycoprotein produced by the syncytiotrophoblast (It can be assayed in maternal blood & urine).
- Low hCG → spontaneous abortion or ectopic pregnancy.
- High hCG → multiple pregnancies or trophoblastic diseases.
- Moore and Persaud, The Developing Human 9th ed

Notes:
Ectopic tubal pregnancy (ETP)
ETP occurs when the blastocyst implants within the uterine tube due to delayed transport.
The ampulla of the uterine tube is the most common site of an ectopic pregnancy. The rectouterine pouch (pouch of Douglas) is a common site for an ectopic abdominal pregnancy.
ETP leads to uterine tube rupture and hemorrhage if surgical intervention (i.e., salpingectomy) is not performed.
ETP presents with abnormal uterine bleeding, unilateral pelvic pain, increased levels of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) (but lower than originally expected with uterine implantation pregnancy), and a massive first-trimester bleed.
ETP must be differentially diagnosed from appendicitis, an aborting intrauterine pregnancy, or a bleeding corpus luteum of a normal intrauterine pregnancy.

## Slide 32: Formation of the Chorion

- A layer of extraembryonic mesoderm proliferates between the embryonic disc and the trophoblast cells. It separates into layers (somatic and splanchnic) to form a space in between which becomes the chorionic cavity. The chorion is a membrane = extraembryonic mesoderm+ syncytiotrophoblast + cytotrophoblast. By the third week, the embryo is suspended from a connecting stalk (umbilical cord) within the chorionic cavity.

Notes:
There is a layer of cells that surrounds the bilaminar embryo and separates it from the trophoblast cells. This layer (extraembryonic mesoderm) proliferates and eventually separates into a layer surrounding the embryo and a layer lining the trophoblast, creating a space, the chorionic cavity. The extraembryonic mesoderm layer and the syncytiotrophoblast and the cytotrophoblast form the chorion.

## Slide 35: Decidua-Functional layer of the Endometrium

- Decidua basalis → Maternal component of placenta (endometrium) where the chorionic villi attach.
- Decidua capsularis → Endometrium that covers the blastocyst, chorion and amnion.
- Decidua parietalis → The uninvolved uterine mucosa.
- PLACENTA = MATERNAL (DECIDUA BASALIS) + FETAL (CHORIONIC VILLI)
- Dudek, RW Board Review Series: Embryology 4th ed

Notes:
Blastocyst implants in the area of the decidua basalis. The endometrium that covers the embedded embryo and expands into the uterine cavity is the decidua capsularis. The other side of the uterus where the embryo has not implanted is the decidua parietalis. As the growing fetus expands in the uterus, the amnion and the chorion fuse together and the decidua capsularis and the parietalis fuse together. The placenta is both maternal and fetal tissue.

## Slide 54

- The notochord induces the overlying ectoderm to differentiate into neuroectoderm & form the neural plate.
- The notochord forms the nucleus pulposus of the intervertebral disc in the adult.
- The neural plate folds to give rise to the neural tube, which is open at both ends at the anterior and posterior neuropores. The anterior and posterior neuropores connect the lumen of the neural tube with the amniotic cavity.
  - The anterior neuropore closes during week 4 (day 25). Failure of the anterior neuropore to close results in upper neural tube defects.
  - The posterior neuropore closes during week 4 (day 27). Failure of the posterior neuropore to close results in lower NTDs (e.g., spina bifida).
- As the neural plate folds, some cells differentiate into neural crest cells.
- The rostral part of the neural tube becomes the adult brain.
- The caudal part of the neural tube becomes the adult spinal cord.
- Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP): AFP levels in maternal blood and amniotic fluid are used to screen for neural tube defects, Down syndrome, and other chromosomal abnormalities.
- Development of the Neural tube

## Slide 62: Lateral Folding Creates the Intraembryonic Body Cavity and Surrounds the Embryo in the Amnion

- Langman's Medical Embryology 14th ed

Notes:
The amnionic cavity is on the dorsum of the embryo. The dorsum of the embryo is covered with surface ectoderm (skin). The ventral side of the embryo is open to the yolk sac. Remember the lateral plate mesoderm and it has 2 layers, the somatic layer which will form the body wall and the visceral layer that covers the viscera/internal organs. The objective is to enclose the embryo in the amniotic cavity and close the ventral body wall. To do that, the layer of lateral plate mesoderm, the somatic mesoderm(body wall) is going to grow ventrally, along with the amniotic cavity, and surround the embryo. A space is formed between the somatic mesoderm and the splanchnic mesoderm that is the other (inside)layer of the lateral plate mesoderm. This space is the peritoneal cavity and the thoracic cavity. The somatic mesoderm will create the ventral body wall and enclose the developing gut tube within the intraembryonic body cavity, leaving in one area only, a connection between the gut tube and the yolk sac that will be part of the umbilical cord.

## Slide 66: Early cardiovascular development

Notes:
The heart tube continues dorsally into a dorsal aorta that passes caudally. Branches of the aortae are (1) umbilical arteries establishing connections with vessels in the chorion, (2) vitelline arteries to the umbilical vesicle, and (3) dorsal intersegmental arteries to the body of the embryo. Vessels on the umbilical vesicle form a vascular plexus that is connected to the heart tubes by vitelline veins. The cardinal veins return blood from the body of the embryo. The umbilical vein carries oxygenated blood and nutrients to the chorion, which, in turn, provides nourishment to the embryo. The arteries carry poorly oxygenated blood and waste products to the chorionic villi for transfer to the mother's blood.

## Slide 71: Clinical Consideration: Amnion and Amniotic Fluid

- Fluid provides protection and allows for free movement
- Fetus swallows about 400 mls (½ total volume) daily
- Increase or decreased fluid volume can be associated with the presence of birth defects
- Polyhydramnios-excess amniotic fluid volume associated with esophageal atresia
- Oligohydramnios-decrease fluid volume associated with renal agenesis
- Amniotic bands - tears in amnion that encircle and constrict developing limbs, can cause deformities
