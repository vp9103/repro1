## Page 13

Primary Ovarian Insufficiency
• Secondary Amenorrhea occurring in patients <40 yrs old
• Follicle dysfunction or depletion
• Incidence – affects 1/100 females
• Symptoms - May have menopause symptoms (ie vasomotor symp,
vaginal dryness)
• Causes – idiopathic, chemo, infections, tumors, autoimmune,
chromosomal
• Diagnosis – lab work (ie FMR1 gene [ risk fragile X], thyroid, etc)
• 2 serum FSH levels in menopause range (>25) at least 1 mon apart
• Treatment – HRT at least until natural menopause (50-51 yo)
• Complications – Bone Health, CV Risk, Dementia

## Page 17

Structural Causes of AUB
• Polyps – benign localized overgrowth of endometrial glands & stroma
• Adenomyosis – benign presence of endometrial glands & stroma w/in
the myometrium of the uterus (not where they are supposed to be)
• Leiomyoma (aka fibroids) – benign smooth muscle tumors of the
myometrium
• Malignancy & Endometrial Hyperplasia
(precancer) – abnormal proliferation of
endometrial tissue
• Usually due to unopposed estrogen
• Must be ruled out for post menopausal
bleeding

## Page 18

Non-structural Causes of AUB
• Coagulopathy – a disorder of one of the components of coagulation
• Ovulatory dysfunction – a disorder of hormonal secretion from the
ovary (ie Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome, etc) or other
hormonal disorder that affects ovarian function (ie thyroid
disorders, diabetes, prolactinomas, etc)
• Endometrial – a dysfunction of the endometrial lining despite
normal hormonal secretion
• Iatrogenic – causes related to medications or medical procedures
(ie hormonal contraceptive therapies, anticoagulants, etc)
• Not Otherwise Classified – causes are poorly understood or rare
(AV malformations, surgical scars, etc)

PMOS (formerly PCOS)
• Diagnosis – lab work (ie thyroid, LH/FSH, AMH, Testosterone, etc)
• Rotterdam Criteria (meet 2/3)
• Clinical or Biochemical Hyperandrogenism
•
Facial & Terminal Hair Growth as evaluated by the Ferriman-Gallwey System

•

•
Progesterone levels

• Excessive number of follicles > 10/plane or >12/ovary in absence of dominant-size follicle of >1.0 cc or

Hypertension, NASH), Infertility, Cancer (Endometrial, Ovarian)

## Page 34

Dysmenorrhea
• Painful menstruation
• Incidence – 50-90%, only about 45% will present
• Usually starts 6-12 mon following menarche
• Risk Factors – age <30, BMI <20, smoking, younger menarche,
longer cycles, heavier flow, and h/o sexual abuse
• Protective Factors – age >30, increasing parity, exercise, hormonal
usage like OCPs
• MOA – Elevated Prostaglandin & Leukotriene levels with
inflammation causing uterine contractions & cramping

• Pelvic Inflammatory Disease

## Page 36

Dysmenorrhea Eval & Treatment
• Symptoms – cramping pain in the lower abdomen/pelvis beginning at
onset of menstrual flow & lasting 8-72 hours
• Primary associated symp: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, headaches, muscle
cramps, low back pain, fatigue, sleep disturbance
• Secondary associated symp: abnormal uterine bleeding, vaginal discharge,
dyspareunia
• Diagnosis – history, abdominal/pelvic exam
• Labwork (ie urine, STI/Vaginosis testing, pregnancy, etc)
• Pelvic ultrasound
• Laparoscopy
• Treatment – NSAIDs, Hormonal Therapy (OCP, leuprolide acetate),
Exercise (45-60 min 3x/wk), TENs, Heat, Acupuncture, Surgery
• Complications – Mood disorders, Missed Work/School, Infertility

## Page 37

Endometriosis
• Presence of endometrial glands & stroma in extrauterine locations
• Estrogen promotes implantation of endometrial tissue & stimulates
inflammation
• Neurogenesis, which is indicated by high levels of nerve growth factors that
alter & increase sensory and sympathetic nerve fibers, is observed in
endometriotic tissue.
• Prevalence – peak is 25-35 yo
• Incidence – most common gyne disorder,
affecting 10% repro age pts
• Risk Factors – increased flow, low BMI, FH,
nulliparity, short cycles, early menarche
Is it Endometriosis?: Evergreen Women's Health, PC.: Board Certified Gynecologists

## Page 38

Endometriosis Eval & Treatment
• Symptoms
• Dysmenorrhea (painful) & Menometrorrhagia (heavy/irregular)
• Dyspareunia
• Chronic pelvic pain
• Infertility
• Cyclical GI symp (ie painful bowel movements)
• Cyclical urinary symp
• Diagnosis – delayed 4-10 yrs
• History & Physical exam – abdominal/pelvic
• Imaging limitations
• Pelvic Ultrasound (85% specificity, 50-84% sensitivity)
• MRI of pelvis +/- abdomen w/o contrast
• Laparoscopy
• Management
• Medications
• Hormonal – suppress estrogen (OCPs, Depo/Nexplanon, etc but less so IUDs), leuprolide acetate
• Analgesics – NSAIDs
• Aromatase inhibitors – Letrozole, etc
• Alternative treatments – acupuncture, exercise, dietary changes (avoid gluten & dairy)
• Laparoscopy – ablation of lesions

Contraceptive Mechanism: Estrogen vs Progestin

• Negative feedback at
hypothalamus/pituitary

Progestin component

ovulation
• Thickens cervical mucus → slows

• Suppresses/atrophies endometrium

## Page 45

Combined Oral Contraceptives (COCs)
Formulation and schedule
• Contain estrogen, most often
ethinyl estradiol, plus a progestin.
• Monophasic: constant
estrogen/progestin dose.
• Triphasic or multiphasic: changing
hormone doses across cycle-
varying progestin does to mimic
cycle
• Typical cycle: active hormone pills
followed by hormone-free interval.
Notes
• Suppress gonadotropins → prevent
ovulation.
• Withdrawal bleeding occurs during
placebo/hormone-free interval.
• Extended-cycle or continuous use
reduces frequency of withdrawal
bleeding.
• Useful for cycle regulation as well
as contraception.

Progestin-Only Contraception
Shared progestin effects
Suppress LH and ovulation to variable
degrees.
Thicken cervical mucus.
Suppress/alter endometrium.
Do not carry the same estrogen-

Shared adverse-effect pattern

Headache, mood symptoms, weight

Androgenic progestins can worsen
acne/hirsutism.

needs and bleeding preferences.

Useful when estrogen is undesirable

Low continuous dose.

## Page 52

Depot Medroxyprogesterone Acetate (DMPA)
High sustained progestin exposure
Mechanism and delivery
• IM/SC Medroxyprogesterone
acetate injection every 3 months.
• Sustained progestin levels strongly
suppress ovulation.
• Many users develop amenorrhea
over time.
Distinctive adverse effects
• Weight gain is common.
• Return to fertility can be delayed for
several months after stopping.
• Bone mineral density loss is the key
long-term adverse effect.

Mifepristone-Progesterone antagonist
Mechanism
Competitive progesterone-receptor
antagonist.
Interferes with progesterone needed to
maintain pregnancy.
Causes decidual
breakdown/separation and cervical
softening.
Increases uterine responsiveness to
prostaglandins.
Adverse effects
Abdominal pain and uterine bleeding are
expected pharmacologic consequences.
Treatment Failure
Incomplete termination can occur.
Mechanistic contraindication:
ectopic pregnancy is not treated by this
regimen.
Also antagonizes glucocorticoid
receptors → explains contraindication in
chronic adrenal failure / long-term
corticosteroid therapy.
