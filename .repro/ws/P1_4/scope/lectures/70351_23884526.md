HSV Infection – General Info
• Virus
• HSV-1 – usually oral infection (herpes labialis or cold sores)
• HSV-2 – usually genital infection (herpes genitalis)
• Transmission
•HSV-1 via oral secretions or sores on the skin, can be spread through kissing or sharing
objects such as toothbrushes or eating utensils.
•HSV-2 generally via sexual contact with someone who has a genital HSV-2 infection.
•It is important to know that both HSV-1 & HSV-2 can be spread even if sores are not
present.
•It can also be transmitted to newborn during a vaginal delivery
• Risks
•Being a women. Women are more likely to have genital herpes than are men. The virus
is sexually transmitted more easily from men to women than it is from women to men.
•Having multiple sexual partners. Each additional sexual partner raises your risk of
being exposed to the virus that causes genital herpes.

## Page 43

The Thymidine Kinase (TK)
• Mechanism of Selectivity:
•Acyclovir remains the "prototypic" antiherpetic. Its selectivity is governed by a
viral "gatekeeper" enzyme:
1. Initial Activation: Acyclovir must be monophosphorylated by viral-encoded
TK. This enzyme is only present in infected cells, preferential activation
occurs in HSV-infected cells.
2. Host Completion: Host cellular kinases then convert the monophosphate into
the active acyclovir triphosphate, which competitively inhibits viral DNA
polymerase and terminates viral DNA elongation.

## Page 44

Valacyclovir
• Pharmacokinetic Optimization
• Acyclovir has poor oral bioavailability; valacyclovir is its L-valyl ester prodrug
with greater oral bioavailability, achieving acyclovir levels comparable to IV
acyclovir and allowing less frequent oral dosing.
• Dosing Strategies
Distinguish between three clinical scenarios:
•First Episode: Intensive, high-dose treatment for initial primary infection.
•Episodic Recurrent: "On-demand" treatment initiated by the patient at the first sign of a prodrome
(tingling/burning).
•Chronic Suppressive: Daily therapy indicated for patients with severe/frequent ≥6 episodes per year. This
strategy significantly reduces both the frequency of outbreaks and the risk of transmission to seronegative
partners.

## Page 45

• IV Acyclovir is excreted unchanged in the urine. If the
drug concentration exceeds its solubility in the renal

• Nephroprotection

## Page 49

Alternative /resistant CMV agents
• Foscarnet: A pyrophosphate derivative. Unlike -cyclovirs, it does
not require phosphorylation by any kinase. It directly binds the
pyrophosphate-binding site of viral DNA polymerase.
•Adverse Effect: Nephrotoxicity. It "chelates" divalent cations, leading to
severe hypocalcemia and hypomagnesemia.
• Cidofovir: A cytosine nucleotide analog. Because it is already a
nucleotide (possesses one phosphate), its activation is independent of
viral enzymes.
•Nephroprotection: Cidofovir is highly nephrotoxic. It must be coadministered with Probenecid (to
block tubular secretion) and Normal Saline hydration.

## Page 55

Principles of Antiviral Selectivity and HIV
Prevention
• Because viruses hijack host metabolic machinery for replication, the
primary challenge is achieving a therapeutic index that spares the
host cell.
• We have shifted from merely managing opportunistic infections
to a strategy of direct viral suppression using Antiretroviral
Therapy (ART).
• This success relies on targeting viral-specific enzymes—such as
Reverse Transcriptase (RT), Protease, and Integrase—that
have no direct functional counterparts in human physiology.

## Page 56

Nucleoside and Nucleotide Reverse
Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs)
• NRTIs, such as Emtricitabine (FTC) and Tenofovir, are the structural
backbone of Antiretroviral Therapy (ART).
• Mechanism of Action:
•NRTIs are analogs of native ribosides that lack a 3'-hydroxyl group.
•To become active, they must undergo intracellular phosphorylation by host cellular enzymes into
triphosphate (or diphosphate for nucleotides) forms.
•Viral RT preferentially incorporates these active analogs into the growing viral DNA strand. Because the
3'-hydroxyl group is missing, the enzyme cannot form the requisite 3',5'-phosphodiester bond with the
next incoming nucleoside. This results in the absolute termination of DNA chain elongation.

## Page 57

NRTIs
• Selectivity and Toxicity:
•While NRTIs have a higher affinity for HIV RT than for host DNA polymerases, Mitochondrial DNA
Polymerase/gamma remains susceptible.
•This cross-reactivity is the primary driver -NRTIs have been associated with potentially fatal lactic
acidosis and hepatomegaly with steatosis

## Page 60

Long-Acting Prophylaxis and Chelation
• Cabotegravir, dolutegravir, and bictegravir are integrase strand
transfer inhibitors (INSTIs); long-acting injectable cabotegravir is
used for HIV PrEP.
• Like all INSTIs and the endonuclease inhibitor Baloxavir, its
mechanism involves the binding of viral enzymes through
the chelation of divalent metal cations.
• Oral INSTIs (e.g., dolutegravir, bictegravir) and baloxavir interact
with polyvalent cations (Ca, Mg, Fe, Al) in antacids/supplements;
baloxavir should also be avoided with dairy products.
• Coadministration leads to drug chelation in the GI tract,
significantly reducing bioavailability.

## Page 61

Additional HIV Agents
• Atazanavir (Protease inhibitor): inhibits HIV protease → prevents viral
polyprotein cleavage and formation of mature infectious virions.
• Atazanavir: requires an acidic environment; take with food; may
cause indirect hyperbilirubinemia/jaundice and PR prolongation.
• Cobicistat is a pharmacokinetic booster—not an antiviral—that inhibits
CYP3A to increase exposure to selected antiretroviral drugs.
•Major issue = drug interactions

Maternal HIV Management
• Preferred: bictegravir or dolutegravir + TAF/FTC, TDF/FTC,
TAF/3TC, or TDF/3TC.
• Bictegravir and dolutegravir are preferred INSTIs in pregnancy;
