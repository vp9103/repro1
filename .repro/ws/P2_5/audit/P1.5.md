## 1. What was built

- **45 tags**, all kebab-case, each with a 2–6-word label. `home` is `{}`. Homes are added in P7.5 / P13.5, and those
  tasks may change only `home` and `labels`, so this list is the closed vocabulary for both waves.
- **Every tag names a way of reasoning, not a topic.** The test for each one: a student who is weak on it would be weak
  on it in at least two topics. The plan's own examples are all present (`feedback-logic`, `binding-proteins`,
  `steroidogenesis-enzymes`, `embryologic-origin`), and so are the reasoning moves the plan lists for the blueprint:
  feedback lab patterns, enzyme-deficiency logic, embryologic origin, tumor markers, drug mechanism to adverse effect,
  teratogen timing, histology to diagnosis, lymph drainage and screening rules.
- **Both waves are covered.** 12 tags are mainly endocrine physiology and labs, 7 anatomy and development, 3 genetics,
  8 pathology and diagnosis, 6 immunity, infection and the fetus, 4 pharmacology, 4 clinical reasoning (work-up,
  cutoffs, screening, treatment order) and 1 ethics (rp29, rp10, rp16). Each of the 48 topics has at least four tags
  that fit its blueprint items. All 45 tags have planned uses in at least two topics (table below), so the ≥ 60 %
  cross-topic rule should hold if taggers pick by reasoning.
- **The label is the category chip** (`rfCatHTML`: "the kind of fact this item tests"), so each label says what kind of
  thinking the item asks for, in plain words and American spelling.

## 3. Tie-break rules for taggers (P5, P6, P7, P11, P12, P13)

An item gets 1–3 tags. The first drives the category chip, so it should be the reasoning the item turns on.

- **Lab pattern that localizes the level of an axis:** `feedback-logic`. **Lab or clue that identifies where excess
  hormone comes from** (tumor, ectopic site, drug): `hormone-source`.
- **Enzyme in the adrenal or gonadal steroid grid:** `steroidogenesis-enzymes`. **Any other synthesis or activation
  step** (thyroid, vitamin D, insulin, catecholamines, T4 to T3, aromatization in fat): `hormone-synthesis`.
- **Receptor type, messenger, or receptor defect:** `receptor-signaling`. **One hormone acting at another hormone's
  receptor:** `receptor-crosstalk`.
- **Insulin, glucagon and glucose:** `fuel-metabolism`, never `hormone-actions`.
- **Endometrial risk explained by estrogen exposure:** `unopposed-estrogen`. **Other risk factors:** `risk-factors`.
- **Which deficit follows a nerve lesion:** `nerve-supply`. **Which structure lies next to or is compressed by
  another:** `anatomic-relations`. **Which vessel, or what ischemia does:** `blood-supply`.
- **Which nodes drain a site:** `lymph-drainage`. **How a tumor spreads or what predicts outcome:**
  `tumor-progression`.
- **Symptomatic patient, next test:** `workup-sequence`. **Healthy population, schedule:** `screening-prevention`.
  **The number itself:** `diagnostic-cutoffs`.
- **How the drug works:** `drug-mechanism`. **What harm follows from that:** `adverse-effects`. **What another drug or
  food does to it:** `drug-interactions`. **Order of therapy:** `treatment-sequence`.
- **What reaches the fetus:** `maternal-fetal-transfer`. **What injury it causes and when:** `teratogen-timing`.
