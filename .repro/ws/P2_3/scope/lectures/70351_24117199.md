## Slide 4: Stages of the Reproductive Cycle

- Prepubertal – the time period between birth and when puberty ends & menses start, fertility is not yet started
  - Ages 0 going up to about 9-14 yrs of age
  - About 1 million follicles are present at birth
- Fertile – the time period in which a pregnancy could occur and is from the start of the first menses to 1 year after the last menses
  - About 10-14 yrs of age going up to about 45-56 yrs of age
  - 250,000-400,000 follicles remain at puberty
- Postmenopausal – 1 year after the last menses until death, fertility has ended during this time period
  - About 45-56 yrs age until death
  - Medium age in the US is 51

## Slide 9: Perimenopause

- Can occur 10 years before an individual is considered postmenopausal
- Hormones fluctuate in irregular patterns causing symptoms
  - Menses can be variable
    - Usually follicular phase is shortened which leads to frequent menses then longer cycles
    - Cycles are anovulatory
  - Vasomotor - hot flashes, night sweats
  - Genitourinary - vaginal dryness, itching, painful intercourse, urinary incontinence, nocturia, frequent UTIs
  - Emotional lability - anxiety, depression
  - Low libido
  - Sleep disturbances - fatigue
  - Cognitive decline - brain fog, memory impairment

## Slide 11: Symptoms of Menopause

- Menses have stopped for 12 months
- Vasomotor Symptoms (VMS) - hot flashes, night sweats
  - 80% of women worldwide
  - Can last 7-8 years
- Genitourinary Symptoms of Menopause (GSM) - vaginal dryness, itching, painful intercourse, urinary incontinence, nocturia, freq UTIs
  - 50% of women worldwide
  - Progressive if left untreated
- Emotional lability - anxiety, depression
  - 70% of women
- Low libido
- Sleep disturbances - fatigue

## Slide 12: Vasomotor Symptoms of Menopause (VMS)

- Upper temp limit decreases so small increases in core temp trigger mechanisms to dissipate heat (ie vasodilation & sweating)
- Lower temp limit increases so small drops in core temp trigger mechanisms to preserve heat (ie shiver & chills)
- Rapid decreases in estrogen
- WITHDRAWAL
- Increased norepinephrine stimulation
- Increased serotonin activation
- Increased central opioid peptide activity
- Narrows the thermoneutral zone (range of core temps) in the thermoregulatory center (hypothalamus)

## Slide 13: VMS cont

- Hot flashes can be unpredictable & occur day & night
- Typically last 3-4 minutes
- Begin with a sensation of hot flushing that spreads across the upper body followed by chills
- Persist for 1-6 yrs most commonly, but can go 15 yrs in 10-15%
- Factors that worsen symp
  - EtOH, Tobacco,
  - Obesity, Sedentary,
  - Emotional Stress, Mood Disorder
  - Black, Lower SES, Lower Edu
- Women with greater VMS burden have
  - More adverse lipid profiles, greater insulin resistance, elevated risk of HTN & DM, great atherosclerosis (carotids, aorta, cerebral small vessels), increased risk of dementia, OSA
- Migraines can be triggered by estrogen fluctuations so they can worsen in early menopause & resolve later in menopause
- When symptoms can affect sleep (ie insomnia), there is an assoc with negative CVD outcomes
- MHT leads to 70-80 % improvement in VMS, relief starts within 2-4 wks

## Slide 14: Genitourinary Symptoms of Menopause (GSM)

- Declining estrogen levels cause thinning of the vaginal mucosa & reduced elasticity via decreased muscle & increased fatty deposits leading to Dryness, Burning, Itching, & Irritation of the vagina
- Urinary frequency, urgency, dysuria, & nocturia can occur as there are estrogen receptors in the bladder & urethra
- Vaginal & Urethral atrophy can occur as well
- Declining estrogen levels lead to less glycogen & lactic acid increasing the vaginal pH (4.5 -> 7.0) promoting bacterial colonization of the vagina
- Increases risk & frequency of UTIs
  - MHT (vaginal estrogen) confers relative risk reduction of 42% in UTIs
- Urinary incontinence & pelvic structure prolapse due to muscle weakness & reduction in ligamentous laxity affecting pelvic floor muscles
- These symptoms can affect sleep
- Vaginal estrogen is 1st line & may be needed even with systemic MHT
  - Vaginal estrogen has little systemic absorption & can be used in women in which system MHT is contraindicated

## Slide 15: Sexual Dysfunction

- Estrogen decline leads to
  - Reduced sexual desire
  - Diminished sexual function
  - GSM causing pain with intercourse
- MHT is not contraception so if there is a pregnancy risk, can use COC or estrogen MHT + Mirena IUD

## Slide 21: Cardiovascular Complications

- Alterations in autonomic regulation

- Impaired arterial endothelial function & renin-angiotensin system activation
- CAD rates 2-3x higher than same aged premenopausal women

## Slide 22: Bone changes

- Osteoporosis increases – thinning of the bones
  - This process can start years before menopause
  - Estrogen decline increases osteoclastic activity compared to osteoblastic activity (so bone is broken down faster than it is rebuilt)
  - Increases risk of fracture
- Joint pain & stiffness
  - Women with longer reproductive life spans have reduced risk of total knee replacements

## Slide 29: Treatments for Menopause Symptoms

## Slide 30: Treatment Considerations

- Consider what symptom(s) need treating & which treatment provides most benefit with least risk
- Initiate hormone therapy near onset of symptoms, delayed initiation of therapy increases risk
  - Generally within 10 years of symptoms onset and ideally younger than 60
  - Risk/Benefit eval for continuing past 60 yrs of age
  - No universal recommendation for when to discontinue HT
- Estrogen-only therapy may reduce risk of breast cancer in women w/o a uterus while Estrogen+Progesterone therapy may increase the risk of breast cancer in women with a uterus
- Smoking with estrogen therapy increases risk
- Nov 2025, FDA recommended removal of black box warning for estrogen therapy for CV disease, breast cancer, and dementia while maintaining warning for endometrial cancer with estrogen only therapy
- Sequential MHT (daily estrogen + progesterone 12-14 days/mon) vs Continuous (combined) MHT
  - Produces predictable withdrawal bleeding with the former vs breakthrough bleeding with the latter (transdermal>oral)

## Slide 31: Treatment Considerations cont

- Bioidenticals have no evidence to suggest superiority & may be riskier as they are compounded in small batches and not regulated for safety or effectiveness
- CBT & Hypnosis – evidence for short term efficacy for VMS & sleep
- Supplements – effective compared to placebo, but not superior to HT & there are concerns for safety due to lack of standardization & regulatory oversight

## Slide 37: Preventative Care Recommendations in Menopause

- Stop smoking, especially if considering MHT/HRT
- Exercise can reduce CV risk (150 min/wk)
- Weight bearing exercise can reduce Osteoporosis risk (2-3 days/wk)
- Heart healthy diet (Mediterranean diet low in saturated fats, DASH)
- Contraception is recommended until menopause (MHT is not contraception)
- Screening tests like Bone Mineral Density, Mammography, Colon Cancer Screening, Cervical Cancer Screening
- Clinician should survey perimenopause/menopause symptoms as not all women will report symptoms

## Slide 39: Women’s Health Initiative - Background

- 1992-2005, recruitment of 161,000 postmenopausal women between 1993-1998 with an age range of 50-79
  - Mean age was 63-64 yrs old
- 40 centers across the US
  - 68,000 in RCTs & 93,676 in observational study
- HT included conjugated equine estrogen (0.625 mg/day) +/- medroxyprogesteone acetate (2.5 mg/day) & measured as it’s end point
  - Coronary Heart Disease
  - Breast Cancer
  - Stroke
  - Pulmonary Embolism
  - Endometrial Cancer
  - Colorectal Cancer
  - Hip Fracture
  - Death from other causes

## Slide 40: Women’s Health Initiative - Outcomes

- Trial stopped early in 2002 because risks were higher than benefits in combined therapy group vs placebo
  - 29% higher CHD risk
  - 41% higher CVA risk
  - 2+ higher risk of PE
  - 26% higher breast cancer risk
  - 37% fewer colorectal cancers
  - 34% fewer hip fractures
- Trial stopped in 2004 for estrogen only arm vs placebo
  - No significant CHD reduction
  - 39% higher CVA risk
  - 39% fewer hip fractures
  - Lower incidence of breast cancer (no defined stat given) & was later found to have significantly lower incidence and mortality
  - No significant total mortality difference
- Lots of confounding factors
  - Age, time since menopause, duration of treatment, baseline CV risk, & hormone types & route of administration

## Slide 41: Women’s Health Initiative - Review

- Average age of women was 63, older than the start of menopause
  - 1/5 to 1/4 were 70-79 yrs old which possibly contributed to increase in CV risk
- Only oral MHT was tested & only 1 estrogen & 1 progesterone
  - Transdermal estrogen has been shown to be safer
  - Multiple doses of each hormone are available & multiple types
  - Current research suggests this matters particularly for VTE risk
- The basis for WHI was to see if HT could prevent disease & did not really test if MHT was effective at treatment of symptoms
  - Don’t use MHT to reduce CV risk or as 1st line for Osteoporosis treatment
  - Timing matters, starting earlier at onset may not increase risks as high
  - Dose, route, and type of hormone also matters

## Slide 43: Postmenopausal Bleeding (PMB)

- Uterine bleeding 12 months or more after their final menses (ie menopause)
- 90% of patients diagnosed with endometrial cancer have PMB
- 2026 updated ACOG guideline
  - Combination of both transvaginal US and endometrial tissue sampling is recommended as part of the initial eval in most patients with PMB
  - Transvaginal US w/o endometrial biopsy may be used during the initial eval of select patients with a single episode of PMB & US showing a fully visualized endometrium that is <4mm in thickness & no risk factors for endometrial cancer, educated on f/u for recurrent bleeding, & no barriers to f/u
- Risk factors - HT, SERM, BMI>30, G0, AA, DM, or genetic (ie Lynch or Cowden syndromes or FH of endometrial cancer), etc

## Slide 44: Guideline Rational for PMB workup

- Incidence increased from 23.5/100,000 women in 2000 to 28.8 in 2022
- Death rate increased from 4.1/100,000 women per year in 2019 to 5.3 in 2023
- Incidence role disproportionately for non-Hispanic Black women at 2.7% per yr through 2022 compared to 0.7% increase for white women
- Death rate in non-Hispanic Black women was 9.8/100,000 women per year compared to 4.8/100,000 women per year in non-Hispanic White
- Technique requires cervical to fundal measurement & measurement could be affected by uterine orientation, adenomyosis, and leiomyomas (the latter assoc with higher incidence in Black women)

## Slide 45: Differential Diagnosis for PMB

- GU Atrophy
- Polyps, either Endometrial or Cervical
- Uterine leiomyomas
- Endometrial Hyperplasia with or without atypia
- Exogenous HT
- Endometrial Cancer (DO NOT MISS!)
- Cervical Cancer
- Other Cancers of the Genital Tract
- Infections – endometritis, cervicitis, vaginitis, cystitis
- Medications – anticoagulants, HT, SERMs
- GI causes or Urinary causes
