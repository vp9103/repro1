- Most common malignant ovarian neoplasm

- Thought to be derived from fallopian tube

- Endometrioid
- Risk factors: endometriosis and Lynch syndrome
- Thought to be derived from endometrium
- Clear Cell

## Slide 11: Malignant Epithelial Ovarian Tumors

- Most common in postmenopausal women
- Comprise >90% of ovarian cancers
- Leading cause of gynecologic cancer related death
- CA-125 is biomarker used for evaluation
- Risk factors:
  - Advanced age
  - Early menarche and late menopause
  - Family history
  - Nulliparity
  - BRCA mutations
- Protective factors:
  - Hormonal contraception use
  - Bilateral salpingectomy or tubal ligation
  - Breastfeeding
  - Multiparity

## Slide 12: BRCA

- BRCA1: 39%–59% of women will develop ovarian cancer
- BRCA2: 11%–20% of women will develop ovarian cancer
- General population: 1.1% of women will develop ovarian cancer
- Risk Reduction:
  - Oral contraceptives (lowers risk by ~50%)
  - Risk reducing bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy after age 35-40

## Slide 16: Sex-cord-stromal Tumors

- Thecoma
- Benign

- Malignant

- Usually adolescents
- Juvenile and Adult types

## Slide 17: Ovarian Torsion

- Twisting of ovary along suspensory ligament (infundibulopelvic ligament) and utero-ovarian ligament results in occlusion of blood vessels to ovary
- Considered gynecologic emergency in order to preserve ovarian function
