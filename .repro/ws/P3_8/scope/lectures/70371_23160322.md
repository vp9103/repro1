## Slide 6: Adrenal cortex

- The adrenal cortex is much larger than the smaller medulla, accounting only for approximately 15% of the gland. It is composed of 3 distinct zones:
- Zona Glomerulosa -- Aldosterone synthesis under ACTH, regulated by Renin
- The zona glomerulosa (outer layer) synthesizes mineralocorticoids, the most important of which is aldosterone. This hormone plays an important role in electrolyte balance and blood pressure regulation.
- Zona Fasciculata --under ACTH control
- The zona fasciculata (middle layer) produces glucocorticoids, the predominant hormone being cortisol. This hormone regulates blood sugar via gluconeogenesis, modulates the immune system, and regulates the metabolism of fat, protein, and carbohydrates. Cortisol secretion is regulated by the adrenocorticotropic hormone, which is released from the pituitary gland.
- Zona Reticularis—under ACTH control
- The zona reticularis (inner zone) produces androgens and plays a role in the development of secondary sexual characteristics. The primary androgen produced in the zona reticularis is dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), which is the most abundant hormone in the body. It serves as a precursor for synthesizing many other hormones the adrenal gland produces, such as progesterone, estrogen, cortisol, and testosterone.

## Slide 8: Adrenal medulla

- The adrenal medulla synthesizes catecholamines.
- Once norepinephrine has been created, it is then methylated via phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase (PNMT), which is only present in the adrenal medulla to epinephrine. In the adrenal gland, norepinephrine and dopamine act mainly as precursor molecules to adrenaline, but in neurons and the central nervous system, they have their own unique roles as important neurotransmitters.

## Slide 12

- The venous drainage from the adrenal glands is dependent on the side of the gland. ## Slide 24: Do not abruptly stop long term steroids

- Withdrawal of Glucocorticoids. The abrupt withdrawal from prolonged use of glucocorticoid drugs like prednisone is the most common cause of AI overall.
- Glucocorticoid drugs act like endogenous cortisol to suppress the HPA axis (lower CRH and ACTH secretion). Over time, the low ACTH levels lead to adrenal atrophy and insufficiency (secondary AI), so the gland cannot make cortisol. As long as patients take the glucocorticoid drug, they will appear normal or have signs of hypercortisolism (Cushing syndrome) like moon facies and mood swings. But, if the drug is abruptly stopped, the suppressed ACTH secretion and insufficient adrenal gland cannot produce enough cortisol to maintain body functions, and signs of hypocortisolism can occur, including nausea, vomiting, hypotension, and hypoglycemia.
- Steroids are anti-inflammatory.

## Slide 26: What is the difference between Cushing Syndrome and Cushing Disease?

- Cushing syndrome is a constellation of symptoms and physical features due to chronic glucocorticoid excess -from any source - pituitary, adrenal, ectopic or exogenous.
- Cushing disease – term is used when cortisol excess is due to excess ACTH secretion from a pituitary source.

## Slide 34: Treatment

- Exogenous (iatrogenic) disease is treated by lowering the glucocorticoid dose or removing the drug, if possible. The drug should be tapered off, not stopped abruptly, to avoid acute adrenal insufficiency.
- Pituitary adenomas can be surgically resected.
- Adrenal adenomas and bilateral adrenal hyperplasia are treated with adrenalectomy, sometimes along with drugs that inhibit the synthesis of cortisol; such medicines include metyrapone, ketoconazole, and mitotane.
- Ectopic cancers are located and treated surgically or with chemotherapy, depending on the cancer. Cortisol-reducing drugs can be used in cancer for unresectable metastatic disease.

## Slide 38

- Genetics
- About 30% to 40% of pheochromocytomas arise from an inherited gene mutation, and another 20% come from a somatic mutation that occurs after birth. Most of these mutations are random, but some happen as part of an inherited syndrome.
- The most common distinct inherited syndromes are the multiple endocrine neoplasia 2 (MEN2) syndrome with RET gene mutations, neurofibromatosis type 1 with NF1 mutations, and the Von Hippel-Lindau disease with VHL mutations. Because of these, genetic testing is an important part of the evaluation of people with pheochromocytoma.

## Slide 48: 21 hydroxylase deficiency

- Genetic Basis:
- The CYP21A2 gene is located on the short arm of chromosome 6. The most common mechanisms leading to defects in this gene are large deletions and gene conversion, which are exchanges of genetic material between CYP21A2 and its pseudogene, CYP21A1P.
- Inheritance Pattern:
- Because it's autosomal recessive, parents who are heterozygous (one normal and one mutated CYP21A2 copy) can be carriers and may not exhibit symptoms themselves. When two carriers have a child, there's a 25% chance of the child inheriting two mutated copies and being affected, a 50% chance of inheriting one normal and one mutated copy and being a carrier, and a 25% chance of inheriting two normal copies and being unaffected.
- Clinical Significance:
- The amount of functional 21-hydroxylase enzyme determines the severity of the disorder. In salt-wasting CAH, the deficiency of aldosterone leads to sodium loss and dehydration, while in simple virilizing CAH, cortisol deficiency leads to androgen excess.
- Diagnosis:
- Very high 17-OHP levels in the blood are a key diagnostic tool, with high levels indicating a potential diagnosis of 21-hydroxylase deficiency. However, comprehensive CYP21A2 genotyping remains the gold standard for confirmation.

## Slide 50: 21 hydroxylase deficiency

- Newborn screening programs that test for 17-hydroxyprogesterone levels, which are elevated in CAH
- Incidence of 21 hydroxylase def - Commonest
- Classic (salt wasting) 1 in 15,000
- Non classic/ Simple virilizing 1 in 1000
- The "salt-wasting" form of the disorder (most severe) is most often associated with large deletions or mutations that result in no enzyme activity. Seventy-five percent of patients with classic 21OHD have this form.
- Twenty-five percent of patients with classic 21OHD have the "simple-virilizing" form (less severe) with low but detectable enzyme activity (1 to 2 percent) that supports some aldosterone production. This form most commonly results from point mutations leading to nonconservative amino acid substitutions such as Ile172Asp. A mutation in intron 2 that variably impairs splicing (g.A/C656G) manifests with a range of classic 21OHD phenotypes.
- Individuals with the nonclassic form (least severe) retain 5 to 20 percent of normal enzymatic activity
- Nonclassic 21OHD may present as early pubarche or sexual precocity in school-age children, hirsutism and menstrual irregularity in young women, or there may be no symptoms.

## Slide 53: Clinical presentation

- 46,XX infants with classic 21OHD are typically born with atypical genitalia characterized by clitoral enlargement , labial fusion, and formation of a urogenital sinus
- Males – 46,XY infants with classic CAH have normal-appearing genitalia at birth but may have subtle findings such as hyperpigmentation of the scrotum or an enlarged phallus.
- Males with the salt-wasting form who are not identified by neonatal screening may present with failure to thrive, dehydration, hyponatremia, hypoglycemia and hyperkalemia typically at 7 to 14 days of life (Adrenal crisis)
- Adults in both – present with early puberty, short stature, fertility issues. Adult male can have testicular adrenal rest tumors

## Slide 54: CAH treatment

- Glucocorticoid therapy – In adults with classic 21OHD, glucocorticoid therapy is required to treat cortisol deficiency, optimize reproductive function and, in females, minimize hyperandrogenic symptoms. Hydrocortisone therapy often achieves these goals and imparts lower risk of long-term complications than longer-acting glucocorticoids.
- Mineralocorticoid therapy – Fludrocortisone therapy is needed to treat mineralocorticoid deficiency
- As with any form of adrenal insufficiency, patients with classic 21OHD should be provided with sick-day rules for stress dosing, medical alert identification, and injectable hydrocortisone sodium succinate (or other injectable glucocorticoid) for emergencies . At each visit, we verify that patients always wear or carry a medical alert identifier and have an unexpired emergency glucocorticoid injection kit.
- Glucocorticoid dose adjustment during acute stress – An increase in glucocorticoid dose is critical during acute physiologic stress including illness or surgery.
