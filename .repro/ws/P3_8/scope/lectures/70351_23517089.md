## Slide 3: Bony Pelvis

- Functions:
- Bears & transfers the weight of axial to the appendicular skeleton.
- Provides attachment for muscles
- Protects pelvic viscera and provides passage of fetus (in females) via the perineum.
- Provides attachment for the erectile bodies of the external genitalia

## Slide 4: Pelvic Ligaments and Foramina

## Slide 5: Pelvic Region

- Pelvic brim separates the false (or greater) pelvis from the true (or lesser) pelvis.
- False (greater) pelvis mostly bounded by the iliac blades; occupied by abdominal viscera.
- True (lesser) pelvis is a bony canal through which the fetus passes during normal childbirth.

## Slide 6: Pelvis & Perineum Boundaries

- The pelvic floor (diaphragm) forms a physical boundary between the pelvic cavity and the perineum.
- The perineum is a diamond-shaped region of the body wall inferior to the pelvic diaphragm.
- Its borders consist of:
- Anterior: inferior border of pubic symphysis
- Anterolateral ischiopubic ramus
- Posteriolateral: Ischial tuberosities & sacrotuberous ligament
- Posterior: tip of coccyx

## Slide 8: Pelvic Measurements

- The diagonal conjugate diameter is the distance between the sacral promontory (palpated by the middle finger) and the anterior surface of the pubic symphysis' inferior margin.
- This distance can be determined during a pelvic examination, which is then used to calculate the obstetrical conjugate. Once the diagonal conjugate is measured, the examiner subtracts 1.5 to 2 cm from the diagonal conjugate measurement.

## Slide 9: Muscles of the Pelvis

- Lateral walls: Obturator internus muscle inserts on the internal surface of the obturator membrane and passes through the lesser sciatic foramen to insert on the greater trochanter of the femur. Within the pelvis, the obturator internus muscle is covered in a thick fascia called the obturator fascia.
- Posterior walls: Piriformis muscle, its proximal attachment is on the anterior aspects of the S2-4 vertebral segments and passes through the greater sciatic foramen to its distal attachment on the greater trochanter of the femur.
- Floor: The pelvic floor (pelvic diaphragm) is a funnel-shaped structure composed of the following muscles: levator ani and coccygeus.

## Slide 28: Prostate

  - Walnut-sized; contributes 20% of ejaculate
  - Produces thin, alkaline fluid that liquefies coagulated semen in the vagina; contains citric acid, proteolytic enzymes, sugars & phosphate.
  - About 3 to 5 ml of semen and 100 million sperm/ml are present in each ejaculation. Ejaculate pH7 - pH8.
  - IUS: Involuntary, smooth muscle, located at the bladder neck, critical for preventing retrograde ejaculation by sealing the bladder outlet during emission.
  - EUS: Voluntary, skeletal muscle, located at the membranous urethra, mainly for urinary control; assists in urethral closure during ejaculation but does not have the primary retrograde flow barrier role.

## Slide 32: Male Reproductive Viscera (Cont.)

- Testis: paired gonads

## Slide 33: Blood Supply of Male Reproductive System

- Arterial supply:
- Testicular a (from aorta)
- Branches of internal iliac aa:
- superior vesical a – gives off a. to the ductus deferens to testes and ejaculatory ducts
- inferior vesical a – branches supply seminal vesicles; prostatic branches to prostate; occasionally will give off a to the ductus deferens
- middle rectal a – branches supply seminal vesicles, prostate
- Venous drainage:
- From testis and epididymis: pampiniform venous plexus to testicular v; Rt testicular v. drains to IVC; Lt testicular v. drains to Left renal Vein.
- From prostate: prostatic venous plexus between the fibrous capsule of the prostate and the prostatic sheath drains into internal iliac veins.
- Other structures (seminal vesicles, ejaculatory ducts): veins join the prostatic and vesical venous plexuses

## Slide 34: The Vertebral Venous Plexus (Batson's plexus)

- A key pathway for the spread of cancer from the pelvic region, including the uterus and bladder, to the spine. This network of valveless veins allows cancer cells to bypass the normal circulatory filtering systems, such as the lungs, and directly access the vertebral column.
- Valveless design: Veins have no valves, allowing blood and cancer cells to move in both directions.
- Pressure changes: Straining or coughing raises belly pressure, pushing prostate blood straight into the spine.
- Direct pathway: Pelvic veins link directly into the Batson venous plexus surrounding the spinal column

## Slide 35: Pelvic Viscera: Distal Urinary Tract

- Ureters: enter posterolateral muscular wall of bladder in oblique fashion, provides sphincter-like action.
- Urinary Bladder: posterior to pubic symphysis
  - Holds ~ 500 mL of urine
  - Trigone of bladder, mesonephric origin
  - Detrusor muscle
  - Sensitive to distention, signals brain the need to empty
- Urethra:
  - Female (3-4 cm)
  - External sphincter: skeletal muscle, voluntary, innervated by somatic fibers in the pudendal nerve (S2-S4).
  - Male (18-20 cm): prostatic (3cm), membranous(2cm), & spongy.
  - Internal sphincter: smooth muscle, involuntary, @ ejaculation, contracts to prevent semen from entering the bladder.
  - External sphincter: skeletal muscle, voluntary, innervated by somatic fibers in the pudendal nerve (S2-S4).

## Slide 37: Pelvic Viscera: Distal GI Tract

- Rectum: terminal part of the large intestine
  - Recto-sigmoid junction @ S3
- Anal Canal: below coccyx
  - Anorectal flexure: anal canal perforates the pelvic diaphragm (levator ani) maintains fecal continence through muscle tone of puborectalis, relaxes during defecation, flexure straightens
  - Internal anal sphincter: smooth muscle, involuntary
  - External anal sphincter: skeletal muscle (deep, superficial and subcutaneous)
  - Pectinate line: Demarcates changes in visceral (above) from somatic (below) portions
