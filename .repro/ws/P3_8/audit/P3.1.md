### 2.4 Facts checked twice (standard Step 1 teaching, ACOG obstetrics, course slides)

- Obstetric conjugate = diagonal conjugate minus 1.5-2 cm and about 10 cm or more is adequate; interspinous is the tightest midpelvic diameter; gynecoid arch wider than 90 degrees.
- Nerve to levator ani: sources differ (course slides S4-S5, blueprint S3-S4, Gray's mainly S4), so the text says "chiefly S4, with S3 and S5".
- Pubococcygeus carries urinary continence (course question key), puborectalis fecal continence; pudendal nerve supplies the external urethral and anal sphincters.
- Ovarian fossa: external iliac vessels above, ureter and internal iliac artery behind, obturator nerve on its floor (medial thigh pain).
- Ureter crosses the pelvic brim at the common iliac bifurcation, under the uterine artery 2 cm lateral to the cervix; ductus deferens crosses over it in men.
- Trigone mesonephric (course slide); bladder about 500 mL; rectosigmoid junction at S3 (course slide).
- Semen: seminal vesicles about 70 percent with fructose, prostate about 20 percent with PSA, liquefaction within about 30 minutes (WHO 15-60); ejaculatory ducts open on the seminal colliculus beside the prostatic utricle;
  emission and bladder-neck closure are sympathetic (L1-L2, alpha-1), erection parasympathetic (S2-S4), ejaculation somatic (pudendal); retrograde ejaculation causes (RPLND, diabetic neuropathy, bladder neck surgery, tamsulosin).
- Membranous urethra is the narrowest and least distensible part apart from the external meatus; spongy urethra torn in a straddle injury below the perineal membrane, membranous in pelvic fracture above it.
- Portocaval anastomosis at the rectum (superior rectal portal, middle and inferior rectal caval); rectal varices are not hemorrhoids.
- Syphilis chancre with painless firm nodes; chancroid and LGV with tender suppurating nodes (bubo); vulvar and penile cancer first to inguinal nodes (sentinel node biopsy).
- Breast: upper outer quadrant is 9-12 o'clock on the right and 12-3 o'clock on the left as the examiner faces the patient; levels I-III relative to pectoralis minor; ipsilateral supraclavicular node is N3c (AJCC);
  casein merocrine, lipid apocrine; Batson plexus links intercostal veins to the vertebral venous plexus. No time-bound numbers were added, so no "as of 2025" wording was needed.
