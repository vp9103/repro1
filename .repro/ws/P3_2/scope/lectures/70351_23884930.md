## Slide 8: Chlamydia – General Info

- Is a genus of bacteria that are obligate intracellular parasites, Chlamydia trachomatis
- Transmitted via oral, vaginal, or anal sex with an infected partner
- Both men & women can contract it
- It can also be transmitted to newborn during a vaginal delivery
- Risk factors include
  - Prior infection with chlamydia
  - Positive for other STIs, esp HIV
  - Have new or multiple sex partners
  - Use condoms inconsistently
  - Do commercial sex work
  - Use illegal drugs
  - Started sexual relationships early in adolescence
  - Live in a detention facility
  - Are a man who has had sex with men
- Most common STI in the US

## Slide 16: Chlamydia – Diagnosis

- Cervical Swab or Vaginal Swab or Urine Sample
- Nucleic Acid Amplification Test (NAAT) is the preferred test
  - Based on amplification of the DNA that is present in Chlamydia trachomatis.
  - Generally more sensitive and specific than other chlamydia tests
  - Can be performed on urine from both men & women
- Other tests include
  - Direct Fluorescent Antibody Stain (DFA), which detects chlamydia antigens, and
  - DNA probe, another test that looks for chlamydia DNA but that is less sensitive than NAAT.
- Chlamydia trachomatis is not visible with Gram stain

## Slide 17: Gonorrhea – Diagnosis

- Cervical Swab or Vaginal Swab or Urine Sample
- Nucleic Acid Amplification Test (NAAT) is the preferred test
  - Based on amplification of the DNA that is present in gonorrhea
  - Generally more sensitive and specific than other culture tests
  - Can be performed on urine from both men & women
- Other tests include
  - Direct Fluorescent Antibody Stain (DFA), which detects gonorrhea antigens, and
  - DNA probe, another test that looks for gonorrhea DNA but that is less sensitive than NAAT.
- N. Gonorrhea is visible on Gram stain
  - Gram-negative intracellular Diplococci

## Slide 20: Chlamydia & Gonorrhea Management

- Treat sexual partner(s) (considered 60 days preceding the pt’s onset of symptoms or diagnosis)
  - Expedited Partner Treatment (EPT) scripts can be written
    - Chlamydia alone - Treat sexual partner(s) w/ oral doxycycline 100 mg 2 x/day x 7 days.
    - Gonorrhea alone - Treat sexual partner(s) w/ cefixime 800 mg as a single dose
- Abstain from intercourse (x1wk) until treatment regimen is completed & symptoms have resolved
- Counsel patient on safe sexual practices & offer testing for additional STIs
- Retesting
  - Test of cure to detect treatment failure (i.e., repeat testing 4 wks after completing therapy) is not advised for nonpregnant persons treated w/ recommended or alternative regimens, unless therapeutic adherence is in question, symptoms persist, or reinfection is suspected.
    - Could lead to false positives as well
    - Most posttreatment infections are the result of reinfection rather than treatment failure
  - Men & women who have been treated for chlamydia should be retested approximately 3 mon after treatment, regardless of whether they believe their sex partners were treated
- Contact CDC/PHD if needed

## Slide 24: Doxy-Pep

- Take doxycycline 200 mg as soon as possible, w/in 72 hours, after anal, oral, or vaginal sex.
- Don't take more than 1 dose of doxy in a 24-hr period, regardless of how many times you have sex in a day.
- You can take Doxy-PEP as often as every day if you need.
- Can help some people reduce chances of getting (STI).
- Patients should see their provider again q 3-6 months.

## Slide 25: Bacterial STI Prophylaxis: Doxy-PEP

- Indication:
  - Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) to prevent bacterial STIs—specifically Chlamydia trachomatis, Syphilis (Treponema pallidum), and to a lesser extent, Gonorrhea—following condomless sexual exposure in high-risk populations.
- Mechanism of Action:
  - Doxycycline is a tetracycline antibiotic.
    - Reversibly binds to the 30S ribosomal subunit, blocking the binding of aminoacyl-tRNA to the mRNA-ribosome complex, thereby inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis (bacteriostatic).
- Adverse Effects:
  - Pill Esophagitis: Can cause severe esophageal mucosal irritation and ulceration. Should be taken with a full glass of water and remain upright for at least 30 minutes before lying down.
  - Photosensitivity: Increased risk of exaggerated sunburns on sun-exposed skin.
  - GI Distress: Nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort.

## Slide 26: Doxy-PEP-Warnings

- Critical Drug Interactions:
  - Avoid taking concurrently with divalent or trivalent cations (calcium, iron, magnesium, aluminum, prenatal vitamins, or antacids). These agents chelate doxycycline in the GI tract, significantly reducing its absorption and systemic efficacy.
  - Pregnancy Warning: Not recommended in pregnancy due to fetal risks, including deposition in calcifying teeth (leading to permanent discoloration) and bone growth inhibition.

## Slide 44: Vulvovaginal Candidiasis - General Info

- Due to a fungus/yeast, Candida Albigans & non-candida albigans species (ie Candida glabrata)
  - Fungal overgrowth due to an imbalance of the naturally occurring yeast in the vagina
- Risk factors include
  - Diabetes or HIV
  - Douching
  - New or multiple sex partners
  - Antibiotics/antifungals or Other Medication Side Effects (think SGLT2i)
  - Using a copper IUD (whereas hormonal IUDs may have a protective effect) or Condoms
  - Frequent Menses
- 40% of all vaginal infection in women of reproductive age & 75% of all women have had one

## Slide 45: Vulvovaginal Candidiasis - Symptoms

- Increased vaginal discharge
  - chunky white discharge
- Itching
- Asymptomatic

## Slide 46: Vulvovaginal Candidiasis - Diagnosis

- pH paper or PhemAlert
  - pH <4.5 (normal vaginal pH)
- Wet Prep/KOH Prep
  - hyphae, pseudohyphae, budding yeast
- Fungal Culture
  - Candida albicans is most common cause
  - can help determine if a different candida species is present & can guide antifungal treatment
- Molecular Testing - PCR

## Slide 47: Vulvovaginal Candidiasis - Treatment

- Oral or Topical antifungals
  - Azoles for Albicans species
    - Clotrimazole & Miconazole are OTC intravaginal agents
      - MOA – alters cell membrane permeability (binding with phospholipids) causing cellular constituents to leak
    - Fluconazole is an oral prescription
      - 150 mg as a single dose
      - MOA – fungistatic - highly selective inhibitor of fungal cytochrome P450 dependent enzyme lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase
  - Non-Azoles for non-Albicans species
    - Nystatin 1 intravaginal suppository (100,000 units) at bedtime x14-30 days
      - MOA – binds to fungal cell membrane sterols disrupting the selective barrier causing cellular constituents to be lost
    - 600 mg of Boric Acid in a gelatin capsule administered vaginally once daily x 3 wks
      - Clinical & mycologic eradication rates of approximately 70%
      - MOA – weak anti-infective agent
- No need to treat sexual partners

## Slide 48: Vulvovaginal Candidiasis in Pregnancy

## Slide 52: Bacterial Vaginosis – Diagnosis

- AMSEL’s Criteria – must have 3 out of 4 present
  - Discharge – thin, homogenous, white-gray, adherent to vaginal walls, milky
  - pH > 4.5
  - KOH “whiff test” is positive
  - Wet prep – Clue Cells >20%, coccoid bacteria, no WBCs, decrease in Lactobacilli
- Sensitivity 37-70% and Specificity 94-99%

## Slide 54: Bacterial Vaginosis – Other Tests

- OSOM BV Blue Test – 10-15 min POC
  - 90.3% sensitivity & 96.6% specificity
- Pap Smear – low sensitivity but high specificity
- Gram Stain – Nugent Score
    - Calculated by assessing for the presence of large Gram + rods (Lactobacillus morphotypes; decrease in Lactobacillus scored as 0 to 4), small Gram-variable rods (Gardnerella vaginalis morphotypes; scored as 0 to 4), & curved Gram-variable rods (Mobiluncus spp. morphotypes; scored as 0 to 2) & can range from 0 to 10. A score of 7 to 10 is consistent w/ BV.

## Slide 55: Bacteria Vaginosis – Treatment

- Clindamycin cream is oil based and might weaken latex condoms and diaphragms for 5 days after use
- Metroniazole MOA - After diffusing into the organism, interacts with DNA to cause a loss of helical DNA structure & strand breakage resulting in inhibition of protein synthesis & cell death in susceptible organisms
- Clindamycin MOA - Reversibly binds to 50S ribosomal subunits preventing peptide bond formation thus inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis; bacteriostatic or bactericidal depending on drug concentration, infection site, and organism

## Slide 56: Bacteria Vaginosis in Pregnancy

- Diagnosis & Treatment before 20 wks of symptomatic BV or those at risk of premature birth is recommended
  - Routine screening of asymp pregnant women is not recommended
- Recommended Regimens
  - Metronidazole 250 mg po tid x 7 days
  - Metronidazole 500 mg po bid x 7 days
  - Clindamycin 300 mg po bid x 7 days
  - Metronidazole gel 0.75% for 5 days
- Recheck in 1 month

## Slide 57: Recurrent Bacterial Vaginosis

- Retest and Rethink
- Use a different recommended treatment regimen
- Retreatment with same recommended regimen is an acceptable approach for treating persistent or recurrent BV after 1st occurrence
- For multiple recurrences, either
  - 0.75% metronidazole gel or 750 mg metronidazole vaginal suppository 2x/wk x >3 mon has been reported to reduce recurrences, although this benefit does not persist when suppressive therapy is discontinued
- Treat Partner (some evidence in same sex couples)

## Slide 58: Recurrent Bacterial Vaginosis cont

## Slide 65: Patient-Applied (Self-Administered) Wart Treatments:

  - Imiquimod: Acts as a Toll-like receptor 7 (TLR7) agonist, inducing local cytokines (Interferon-\alpha, TNF-\alpha) to stimulate a cell-mediated immune response. External application.
  - Podophyllotoxin: A purified mitotic inhibitor that causes metaphase arrest in dividing cells. External application. Contraindicated in pregnancy.

## Slide 66: Clinician-Administered Wart Treatments:

  - Trichloroacetic acid (TCA) / Bichloroacetic acid (BCA): Caustic agents that chemically cauterize the lesion. These are preferred in pregnancy as they have no systemic absorption.
  - Podophyllum Resin: A crude, non-purified version of the mitotic inhibitor. Requires a mandatory wash-off within 1–4 hours of application to prevent systemic neurotoxicity.
  - Intralesional interferon-α: Reserved for refractory genital warts; limited by adverse effects and poor tolerability. Induces host cell enzymes that inhibit viral RNA translation and degrade viral mRNA.
  - Procedurally, these can also be frozen with liquid nitrogen, cauterized off at the base, or treated with a laser.

## Slide 67: Inflammatory - Vaginal

- Desquamative inflammatory vaginitis – uncommon chronic purulent vaginitis occurring mostly in perimenopausal Caucasians
  - Symp – abnormal discharge
  - Diagnosis of exclusion – on microscopy, increase in inflammatory & parabasal epithelial cells (immature squamous cells), pH >4.5
  - Treatment with topical clindamycin or corticosteroids
- Atrophic vaginitis / Genitourinary syndrome of menopause – hypoestrogenic state
  - Symp – decreased vaginal lubrication, dyspareunia, burning, pressure, itching, change in discharge, freq infections, pale vulvar appearance, loss of vaginal rugae, fusion of labia minora, introital stenosis, bleeding
  - Diagnosis – r/o infections, chemical irritants, peri/menopause
  - Treatment with vaginal estrogen & moisturizers

## Slide 68: Inflammatory - Vulvar

- Lichen sclerosis – chronic inflammatory
- dermatitis affecting the vulva & anogenital
- regions (figure of 8 pattern)
  - Symp – white atrophic plaques, intense pruritus, dyspareunia, dysuria, labia minora regression & clitoral scarring
  - Risk of progression to SCC
  - Treatment with ultrapotent topical steroids
- Lichen planus – T cell–mediated inflammatory autoimmune condition affecting skin & mucosa that can affect genital & oral regions
  - Symp – pain, burning, scarring, well-demarcated bright-red vestibular patches with a hyperkeratotic border or a net-like plaque known as Wickham striae
  - Risk of scarring and developing vaginal stenosis if untreated
  - Treatment with ultrapotent topical & intravaginal suppositories of steroids, may need vaginal dilatation

## Slide 69: Inflammatory – Vulvar cont

- Lichen simplex chronicus – itch-scratch cycle causing thickened leathery skin usually due to underlying eczema, asthma, or allergies usually in 60 yo+ or those with anxiety disorders
  - Symp – intense pruritus even disturbing sleep, excoriations on vulva
  - Treatment is breaking the cycle with steroids
- Contact dermatitis (irritant and allergic)
- Psoriasis of the vulva
- Hidradenitis suppurativa – inflammatory skin condition/folliculitis due to increased cytokine & chemokine secretion resulting in hyperkeratosis, follicular occlusion, & cyst development in apocrine gland–bearing skin (axilla, pubic)
  - Symp - painful lumps & tunnels leading to scarring & recurrent abscesses, open double “tombstone” comedones
  - Risk – female, black, FH, tobacco, obesity, DM, Crohn’s
  - Diagnosis – clinical, Hurley classification staging system (I-III)
  - Treatment – loose clothing, lose weight, stop tobacco, topical clindamycin, oral tetracyclines, spironolactone, biologics, surgery

## Slide 70: Other Vulvar Pathologies

- Vulvodynia – vulvar pain without an identifiable cause that persists for at least three months (diagnosis of exclusion)
  - Can occur alone or in association with another disorder like interstitial cystitis, fibromyalgia, irritable bowel syndrome, pelvic myofascial pain, psychosocial conditions, & sexual dysfunction
  - Symp – localized or generalized pain, random or provoked, intermittent or constant
  - Most common cause of dyspareunia in premenopausal (12% prevalence)
  - Diagnosis – pressure point testing using a cotton swab in a circumferential fashion along the vulvar vestibule, which will elicit pain with even light touch in a patient with vulvodynia
  - Multimodal treatment – pelvic floor physical therapy, cognitive behavioral therapy, neuropathic pain modulators, vestibulectomy
