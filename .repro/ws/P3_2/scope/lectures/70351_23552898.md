Learning Objectives
• Describe the locations and relationships between the true pelvic cavity, the false pelvis, and the
perineum.
• Identify the features of the bony pelvis that are important for adequacy of the pelvis for childbirth (pelvic
inlet via diagonal conjugate, interspinous distance, intertuberous distance, width of subpubic arch).
• Explain the function of the pelvic floor, its innervation, and the two major muscles that comprise it.
• Describe the peritoneal relationships of the pelvic viscera, including pouches and spaces.
• Describe the anatomical features and general functions of the pelvic viscera in females (bladder, uterus,
uterine tube, ovary, cervix, vagina, and rectum).
• Describe the anatomical features and general functions of the pelvic viscera in males (bladder, ductus
deferens, seminal vesicle, ejaculatory duct, prostate, prostatic urethra, and rectum).
• Trace the arterial blood supply and venous drainage of internal pelvic structures to their vascular origins
and terminations.
• Relate the pelvic vasculature to neighboring organs, fascial planes, and clinically significant anatomical
landmarks.
• Apply anatomical knowledge explaining methods of achieving hemostasis during common gynecologic,
urologic, and colorectal surgical procedures.

Pelvic Measurements
• The diagonal conjugate diameter is the distance between the sacral promontory
(palpated by the middle finger) and the anterior surface of the pubic symphysis'
inferior margin.
• This distance can be determined during a pelvic examination, which is then used to
calculate the obstetrical conjugate. Once the diagonal conjugate is measured, the
examiner subtracts 1.5 to 2 cm from the diagonal conjugate measurement.

Internal iliac artery

## Page 48

The uterosacral ligaments and the transverse cervical (cardinal) ligaments are the two main
ligaments stabilizing the uterus.They help to inhibit the uterus from prolapsing into the
vagina.
The round ligament of the uterus is related to the descent of the ovaries in embryologic
development and continues into the inguinal canal. This ligament is the remnant of the
gubernaculum.
The broad ligament is the peritoneal covering over the uterine tubes, uterus, and vessels.
he arcus tendineus fascia pelvis joins the muscle fascia of the levator ani to the pubocervical
fascia on the vagina and is not directly associated with the uterus or its ligaments. The
levator ani muscles contribute to the floor of the pelvis and support all of the pelvic viscera
indirectly; it does not, however, prevent prolapse of the uterus into the vagina.

## Page 57

The Vertebral Venous Plexus (Batson's plexus)
A key pathway for the spread of cancer from the pelvic
region, including the uterus and bladder, to the spine.
This network of valveless veins allows cancer cells to
bypass the normal circulatory filtering systems, such as
the lungs, and directly access the vertebral column.
•
Valveless design: Veins have no valves, allowing blood
and cancer cells to move in both directions.
•
Pressure changes: Straining or coughing raises belly
pressure, pushing prostate blood straight into the spine.
•
Direct pathway: Pelvic veins link directly into the Batson
venous plexus surrounding the spinal column.
