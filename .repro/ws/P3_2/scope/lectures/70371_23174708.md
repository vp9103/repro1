## Slide 8: Insulin – Anabolic hormone

- The normal fasting glucose level is 60 to 100 mg/dL. Maintaining this normal level is important
- After a glucose load, the level of blood glucose increases. The excess glucose signals the endocrine pancreas to secrete insulin, which promotes glucose storage in the liver and muscles. This stored glycogen is the primary short-term storage form of glucose.
- When the liver is saturated with glycogen, any additional glucose taken up by hepatocytes is instead converted to fatty acids, which enter the blood as lipoproteins. These can provide free fatty acids for adipocytes. The adipocytes are stimulated by insulin to take up glucose and convert it to glycerol. In the adipocyte, this glycerol combines with the free fatty acid to make triglycerides, stored in the adipocyte for future energy use. So insulin leads to storage: liver glycogen and adipocyte triglycerides.

## Slide 11: Glucose uptake

- Autophosphorylation of tyrosine residues starts the signaling pathway, which ends with glucose transporter type 4 (GLUT4) moving to the cell membrane. This allows glucose to enter the cell by using facilitated diffusion down its concentration gradient. (Intracellular glucose levels are very low, since glucose is quickly metabolized). This pathway stimulates the formation of glycogen, lipids, and proteins in multiple cell types. Separately, another insulin-dependent pathway, the mitogen-activated protein (MAP) kinase pathway, promotes cell growth and DNA synthesis.

## Slide 12: Glucagon is catabolic Glucagon stimulates glycogenolysis. Glucagon stimulates gluconeogenesis – production of glucose from non carbohydrates.

- Glycogenolysis. Opposing the function of insulin, glucagon performs glycogenolysis—the breakdown of stored glycogen to provide glucose (and energy). This mainly occurs in the liver, where glucagon receptors are dense.

## Slide 21: Classification

- 1. Type 1 diabetes (due to autoimmune β-cell destruction, usually leading to absolute insulin deficiency, including latent autoimmune diabetes in adults)
- 2. Type 2 diabetes (due to a nonautoimmune progressive loss of adequate β-cell insulin secretion, frequently on the background of insulin resistance)
- 3. Specific types of diabetes due to other causes, e.g., monogenic diabetes syndromes, diseases of the exocrine pancreas, and drug- or chemical-induced diabetes
- 4. Gestational diabetes mellitus (diabetes diagnosed in the second or third trimester of pregnancy that was not clearly overt diabetes prior to gestation or other types of diabetes occurring throughout pregnancy, such as type 1 diabetes).
- AABBCC approach: Age (e.g., for individuals <35 years old, consider type 1 diabetes); Autoimmunity (e.g., personal or family history of autoimmune disease or polyglandular autoimmune syndromes); Body habitus (e.g., BMI <25 kg/m); Background (e.g., family history of type 1 diabetes); Control (preferred term is “goal,” i.e., the inability to achieve glycemic goals on noninsulin therapies); and Comorbidities (e.g., treatment with immune checkpoint inhibitors for cancer can cause acute autoimmune type 1 diabetes)
- Loss of pancreas tissue- pancreatitis, surgery etc – use insulin for therapy

## Slide 23: Autoimmunity in type 1

- Type 1 diabetes is immune- related in 95% of diabetes and is caused by autoimmune destruction of the pancreatic β-cells. Highest incidence in Scandinavians / Northern European descent.
- Autoimmune markers include islet cell autoantibodies and autoantibodies to glutamic acid decarboxylase (GAD) (such as GAD65- seen in 75%), insulin, the tyrosine phosphatases islet antigen 2 (IA-2) and IA-2b, and zinc transporter 8 (ZnT8).
- Cromosome 6 short arm- The disease has strong HLA associations, with linkage to the DQB1 and DRB1 haplotypes, and genetic screening has been used in some research studies to identify high-risk populations. Specific alleles in these genes can be either predisposing (e.g., DRB1*0301-DQB1*0201 [DR3-DQ2] and DRB1*0401-DQB1*0302 [DR4-DQ8])
- Type 1 diabetes can be associated with polyglandular autoimmune syndromes-Hashimoto thyroiditis, Graves disease, celiac disease, Addison disease, vitiligo, autoimmune hepatitis, myasthenia gravis, and pernicious anemia

## Slide 32: Metabolic syndrome

- Metabolic syndrome is a cluster of medical conditions that increases the risk of heart disease, stroke, and type 2 diabetes.
- It's diagnosed when a person has at least three of the following:

## Slide 34: Symptoms

- What are common symptoms for diabetes?
- Polyuria – due to osmotic diuresis (glucose in blood attracts water; excess glucose is removed from blood by the kidneys and excreted via the urine, along with water and electrolytes) = hyperosmolar state (loss of water and high glucose/sodium)
- Polydipsia – excessive thirst due to hyperosmolar state/ water loss
- Polyphagia – increased hunger; due to lack of insulin and ability for glucose to enter cells. Body cannot convert glucose into usable source of energy.
- Weight loss – despite normal or increased appetite; due to depletion of water, glycogen( glucose stores); skeletal muscle catabolism and conversion of amino acids to glucose via gluconeogenesis in the liver
- Dizziness and weakness – due to dehydration, low BP, whole body potassium loss
- High ketones contribute to nausea and vomiting (cause unknown), leading to decreased food and fluid intake, exacerbating hyperosmolar state. Ketones also result in sweet, fruity breath odor and increased respiratory compensation (Kussmaul respiration)

## Slide 64

- During DKA, much of it shifts out into the blood, causing hyperkalemia. This happens for three main reasons:
- Insulin normally stimulates the Na+/K+-ATPase to pump potassium from the blood into cells, so a lack of insulin means this pump is inactive, raising the potassium level in the blood.
- Hyperglycemia increases the serum osmolarity, pulling water out of all cells, and the potassium follows along (osmotic shift).
- To buffer the decreased serum pH, the membrane H+/K+ exchanger brings extracellular H+ into the cell and pumps K+ out.
- So, the total level of potassium is low in the patient’s body, but the initial level in the serum is high.
