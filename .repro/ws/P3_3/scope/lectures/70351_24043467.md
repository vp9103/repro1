3. Endometrial Polyp
Endometrial Polyp
simple cystic hyperplasia with dilated glands in loose stroma
•
Endometrial polyps are abnormal growths of
the endometrium
•
either sessile (a non-mobile polyp with a
broad base with no stalk)
or pedunculated (projecting into the uterine
cavity and attached by a stalk).
•
Histologically, they resemble the stratum
basalis of the endometrium
(i.e., cystically dilated glands with small
muscular arteries)
•
The endometrial polyp stroma is
more collagenous than the normal
endometrial stroma (more cellular)
•
diagnosed in the presence of 2 of the
following 3:
•Thick-walled vessels
•Collagenous stroma
•Epithelium on at least 3 sides
•
Endometrial polyps usually present with
abnormal uterine bleeding.
•
Although malignant transformation is rare,
Endometrial carcinoma can arise in
endometrial polyps

4. Non-atypical Endometrial Hyperplasia:
•
Closely packed tubular, irregular or branching glands
•
lined by pseudostratified columnar epithelium resembling
proliferative phase endometrium.
•
There is no cytologic atypia.
Compare To
Atypical Endometrial Hyperplasia:
Atypical Endometrial Hyperplasia/Endometrioid
Intraepithelial Neoplasia (AEH/EIN): The glands are
arranged back-to-back with little intervening stroma
(gland-to-stroma ratio >1). The glandular profiles
are round and tubular or have complex, stellate or
irregular shapes with branching and outpouchings.
Cytologic atypia is characterized by large round or
elongated nuclei with high N:C ratio, loss of nuclear
polarity with irregular distribution of nuclei, and
inconspicuous nucleoli. Some cases show distinct
nucleoli. Mitotic activity is variably increased.

6. Leiomyoma (Fibroid)
Gross:
• Sharply circumscribed, firm gray-white masses with a
characteristic whorled cut surface
• Embedded within the myometrium (intramural), lie beneath
the endometrium (submucosal) or the serosa (subserosal;
may extend out on attenuated stalks (pedunculated)
Microscopic:
• Bundles of intersecting uniform smooth
muscle cells (elongated nuclei and
eosinophilic cytoplasm)
• Similar in appearance to typical myometrium
• Foci of fibrosis (collagen bundles),
calcification, and degenerative softening may
be present

## Page 19

I. Rete Testis
•
Rete testis is an anastomosing network of delicate
channels located in the testicular hilum.
•
It is lined by flattened squamous or low cuboidal
epithelium. Epithelium-covered strands and columns
seen in the photomicrograph are called chordae retis
which connect the opposing walls of the channels.
•
Rete receives the luminal contents of seminiferous
tubules and empties them into the efferent ductules.
