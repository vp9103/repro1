1 [e] This largest chancre is a shallow, red ulcer with a sharply defined edge and smooth base. Its moist surface contains many spirochetes. Histologically, a dense plasma cell infiltrate and obliterative endarteritis in the underlying dermis create the characteristic firm, 'indurated' feel on palpation.
2 [e] The middle lesion shows a round, moist erosion with a pale center and red rim. While a single painless ulcer is the classic presentation of primary syphilis, multiple chancres can occur if Treponema pallidum is inoculated into several small abrasions simultaneously during sexual contact.
3 [e] This lower, elongated erosion sits on otherwise healthy-appearing skin. Each of these lesions represents a primary site of bacterial entry. Even without antibiotic treatment, these chancres will typically heal spontaneously within 3 to 6 weeks, though the infection will progress to the secondary stage.
