1 [arrow] Thin epidermis: a narrow, flattened ribbon of squamous cells runs across the top with loss of the rete ridges, which explains the thin, wrinkled, parchment-like white skin seen in the clinic.
2 [r] Homogenized dermal collagen: directly beneath the epidermis, a broad, pale pink band of collagen has lost its fiber pattern and almost all of its cells; this sclerosis gives the white color and later scarring.
3 [e] Lymphocytic infiltrate: below the pale zone, small dark lymphocytes cluster in the deeper dermis, most clearly at the lower left; this T-cell mediated inflammation causes the itching and drives the scarring.
