1 [e] Large polygonal tumor cells with abundant clear, glycogen-rich cytoplasm and a round central nucleus create the classic 'fried-egg' appearance. Glycogen dissolves during routine slide preparation, leaving the distinctive pale halo.
2 [e] Nests of tumor cells are separated by thin fibrovascular septa containing prominent infiltrates of small, dark, mature lymphocytes. This T-cell-rich immune response is a signature histological feature of testicular seminoma.
3 [e] Neighboring cells maintain crisp, well-defined cytoplasmic membranes that produce a sharp polygonal mosaic. This orderly architecture directly contrasts with the messy, overlapping, syncytial borders of embryonal carcinoma.
4 [e] Nuclei throughout the tumor nests share a remarkably uniform size, round contour, vesicular chromatin, and prominent central nucleoli, reflecting homogeneous germ-cell differentiation rather than the extreme pleomorphism of embryonal carcinoma.
