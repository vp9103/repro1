1 [none] Crowded glands: Tightly packed glands fill most of the field, so the gland-to-stroma ratio is higher than in normal proliferative endometrium, the defining feature of hyperplasia driven by unopposed estrogen.
2 [e] Variable gland size and shape: Some glands are large and dilated while others are small, round or branching, which gives the architectural complexity that earns the name complex hyperplasia; a large dilated gland sits at the upper left.
3 [arrow] Bland nuclei: The cells lining the glands are small, oval and uniform, without enlarged rounded nuclei or prominent nucleoli, so there is no atypia and the cancer risk is low, about 1 to 3 percent.
4 [arrow] Intervening stroma: Bands of cellular endometrial stroma still separate neighboring glands almost everywhere, which distinguishes hyperplasia from carcinoma, where glands fuse back to back with no stroma between them.
