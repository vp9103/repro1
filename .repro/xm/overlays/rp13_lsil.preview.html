1 [e] Koilocyte: This large squamous cell displays a prominent, sharp perinuclear halo (cleared cytoplasm) and a condensed, dark peripheral rim of cytoplasm. This characteristic cytopathic change is caused by productive HPV infection, defining LSIL.
2 [arrow] Nuclear enlargement: The nucleus of the koilocyte is several-fold larger than normal, showing coarse, dark chromatin and an irregular nuclear membrane, reflecting active viral replication and genomic instability.
3 [e] Binucleate cell: Two enlarged nuclei sit side-by-side within a single cleared cytoplasmic space. Binucleation is another classic cytopathic effect of HPV infection commonly observed in LSIL.
4 [e] Normal squamous cells: These mature cells (pink superficial and blue-green intermediate) have abundant, flat cytoplasm and tiny, round, condensed nuclei. They serve as an internal size benchmark to highlight the abnormal nuclear enlargement in koilocytes.
