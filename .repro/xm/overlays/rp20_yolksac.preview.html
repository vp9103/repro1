1 [e] Schiller-Duval body: this signature structure features a papilla with a central vessel lined by tumor cells projecting into a surrounding space, mimicking a renal glomerulus. It is pathognomonic for yolk sac tumor, the most common testicular tumor in children under 3 years old.
2 [r] Papillary architecture: tumor cells form branching papillae supported by central fibrovascular cores, interspersed with lacy, reticular spaces. This lacy network is the predominant histological growth pattern in yolk sac tumors.
3 [arrow] Lining epithelium: papillae are lined by crowded, primitive cuboidal tumor cells with prominent nuclei and high N:C ratios. These neoplastic cells synthesize alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), causing markedly elevated serum AFP levels.
