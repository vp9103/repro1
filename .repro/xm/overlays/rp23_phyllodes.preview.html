1 [e] Leaf-like fronds: broad, rounded lobes of stroma bulge into cystic spaces, producing the classic 'leaf-like' architecture that names the tumor. These expansile projections compress adjacent ductal structures.
2 [arrow] Epithelial lining: a thin, dark purple layer of benign epithelium lines the cleft and drapes over the frond. This double-layered epithelium is non-neoplastic, serving as a passive covering over the proliferating stroma.
3 [e] Cellular stroma: the frond interior consists of proliferating spindle cells with mild to moderate cellularity. Stromal cellularity, cytologic atypia, and mitotic rate distinguish benign phyllodes from borderline and malignant forms.
4 [e] Long clefts: compressed, elongated ductal spaces lined by epithelium snake between adjacent stromal fronds. These elongated cleft-like spaces are a hallmark finding separating phyllodes tumor from fibroadenoma.
