1 [arrow] The two central incisors feature a distinct central notch or crescentic dip along their biting edge instead of a normal flat contour. Intrauterine infection with Treponema pallidum destroys the central enamel organ of the tooth germ during development, creating these hallmark notched Hutchinson teeth.
2 [arrow] A prominent central gap (diastema) separates the narrow, peg-shaped central incisors. Because the teeth taper towards their biting edges rather than widening normally, broad spacing remains between them instead of normal interproximal contact.
3 [arrow] These changes affect the permanent dentition, making Hutchinson teeth a late manifestation of congenital syphilis that becomes evident only upon tooth eruption in childhood. They represent one component of Hutchinson's classic triad, alongside Eighth nerve deafness and interstitial keratitis.
