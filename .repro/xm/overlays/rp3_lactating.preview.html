1 [e] In the left panel, swollen, closely packed lobules fill the field, separated only by thin pink septa. This dramatic lobular hyperplasia is driven by estrogen, progesterone, and prolactin during pregnancy to prepare for lactation.
2 [e] Along the upper left edge, a thin rim of adipose tissue and fibrous septa separates the lobules. In a resting, non-lactating breast, dense fibrous stroma and fat would dominate the histology, with only scattered, inactive ducts.
3 [e] In the right panel, the cuboidal epithelial cells lining the alveolus contain clear lipid-filled vacuoles and bulge at the apical surface. These lipids are released into the lumen via apocrine secretion, where the apical membrane pinches off around the lipid droplet.
4 [e] In the right panel, pink granular secretion (milk) fills the alveolar lumen. Milk proteins, such as casein, are synthesized in the rough endoplasmic reticulum and secreted via merocrine secretion (classic exocytosis of secretory vesicles).
