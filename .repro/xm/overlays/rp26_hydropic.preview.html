1 [arrow] Hydropic villus: a large rounded villus with pale, loose, edematous stroma and a central acellular cistern sits at the center, swollen by fluid. This is the microscopic form of the grape-like vesicle seen on gross inspection.
2 [e] Trophoblastic hyperplasia: along the upper and right border of the villus, layers of proliferating trophoblast with enlarged, atypical nuclei and vacuolated cytoplasm form a collar around it, reflecting paternal genes that drive trophoblast growth.
3 [c] Absent fetal vessels: the swollen stroma is loose and nearly acellular with no visible capillaries, because the embryo failed to form and no fetal circulation supplies the villus.
4 [r] Hemorrhage: a dense pool of red cells at the left edge, with fragments of villi, reflects the vaginal bleeding that brings most patients to care.
