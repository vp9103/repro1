1 [e] Multinucleated cluster, left center: several large nuclei share one syncytial cytoplasm because infected cells fuse. These multinucleated giant cells are what a Pap smear or Tzanck smear shows in herpes infection.
2 [arrow] Molded nuclei: inside the cluster the nuclei press against one another and take on flattened, interlocking edges, called nuclear molding, because crowding in the shared cytoplasm deforms them.
3 [arrow] Ground-glass nuclei: the infected nuclei look pale, smudged and glassy, with chromatin pushed to a thickened rim along the nuclear membrane, because viral replication fills the nucleus and disperses the chromatin.
4 [e] Normal squamous cells: the flat blue-green cells at the top center and on the right have small, crisp, single nuclei and give a size comparison, since the infected nuclei are several times larger and overlap.
