1 [e] Glistening pink-white smooth muscle bundles interlace and sweep in swirling, concentric whorls across the cut face. This classic whorled appearance reflects the organized fascicular growth of benign smooth muscle cells and represents the gross hallmark of a leiomyoma.
2 [arrow] The cut surface terminates in a distinct, smooth, rounded boundary rather than invading adjacent tissue. Because leiomyomas are benign, non-infiltrative tumors with pushing borders, surgeons can readily enucleate ('shell out') the nodule while sparing surrounding uterine myometrium.
3 [arrow] The parenchyma is uniformly tan-white, firm, and glistening without mottled softening, dark hemorrhage, or cystic necrosis. In contrast, malignant leiomyosarcomas exhibit high cellular turnover and vascular fragility, producing a soft, variegated, fleshy surface with prominent hemorrhage and necrosis.
4 [arrow] The unsectioned left half reveals a rounded, rougher exterior with fine fibrous tags. These adhesions mark the surgical cleavage plane where the benign pseudoencapsulated nodule was peeled away from the surrounding muscular wall of the uterus.
