1 [e] This large polymorphonuclear neutrophil contains dozens of tiny, pink-magenta, paired cocci (diplococci) within its cytoplasm. Seeing these intracellular organisms in a purulent specimen is highly characteristic of Neisseria gonorrhoeae.
2 [e] Another neutrophil shows the same intracellular pink-magenta diplococci. The pink color indicates they are gram-negative, having a thin peptidoglycan layer that does not retain the crystal violet stain during decolorization.
3 [arrow] A few diplococci lie outside the cells in the extracellular space. While most gonococci are intracellular during acute infection, some free organisms are commonly observed in the background of the smear.
4 [arrow] The field is crowded with numerous uninfected, multilobed neutrophils. Neisseria gonorrhoeae infection triggers a robust, purulent inflammatory response, resulting in an abundance of polymorphonuclear leukocytes in the discharge.
