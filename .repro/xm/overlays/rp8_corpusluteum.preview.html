1 [arrow] The thick, scalloped band of bright yellow tissue is the corpus luteum. Its characteristic yellow color is due to lipid-rich granulosa-lutein and theca-lutein cells, which store cholesterol used for progesterone synthesis.
2 [arrow] The pale center of the yellow band contains a dark red blood clot and fibrin. This represents the remnant of the ruptured follicle's cavity, which fills with blood immediately after ovulation.
3 [arrow] These white, wrinkled, fibrous streaks in the ovarian stroma are corpora albicantia. They represent degenerated, scarred corpora lutea from previous menstrual cycles.
