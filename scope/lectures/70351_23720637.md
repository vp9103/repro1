## Slide 3: Perineum

- The urogenital triangle is the anterior triangular region of the diamond-shaped perineum.
- In anatomical position, it is oriented in a horizontal plane & contains the urethra and external genitalia.
- The anal triangle is the posterior triangular region
- In anatomical position, It is oriented in a plane that is almost vertical and contains the anal canal, anus, external anal sphincter, inferior rectal nerve (branch of pudendal nerve), and the fat-filled ischiorectal fossa.

## Slide 4: Urogenital Triangle: Perineal Membrane

- Perineal membrane: thin fibromuscular horizontal layer with fibers running parallel to pelvic diaphragm (i.e., levator ani), divides urogenital triangle into a deep (superior to membrane) & superficial (inferior to membrane) pouch

## Slide 7: Ischioanal (Ischiorectal) Fossa

- Fossa filled with fat, poorly vascularized.
- Allows for expansion of the anal canal during defecation & accommodates the fetus during childbirth.
- Infections from one of the fossae can spread to the other & into the urogenital triangle.
- Contents: Fat, pudendal nerves and vessels giving the inferior rectal nerve & vessels

## Slide 10: Female & Male Superficial Pouch

- Male: Bulb & crura (attached) of the penis give rise to erectile sinuses of the penis – corpus spongiosum & corpora cavernosa (free), respectively.
- Female: Bulb of the vestibule & crura (attached) give rise to erectile sinuses of clitoris – glans clitoris & corpora cavernosa (free) respectively.

## Slide 11: Female & Male Superficial Pouch: Erectile Tissue

- Root/attached components: sit/attach to the perineal membrane & are the bulb and crus.
- Free components: corpora cavernosa - body of the clitoris (F) & body of the penis (M), and corpus spongiosum –glans clitoris (F) and glans penis (M).

## Slide 12: Male Superficial Pouch

- Superficial pouch contains external genitalia & associated skeletal muscles: bulbospongiosus, ischiocavernosus, & superficial transverse perineal muscles. Because of their action during urination/erection, these muscles are generally more developed in males.
- Sup. transverse perineal muscles: provide a firm base for the penis during erection
- Bulbospongiosus: aids in emptying of urine/semen.
- Ishiocavernosus: force blood from crura to corpora cavernosa, increasing turgidity of penis.

## Slide 13: Female Superficial Pouch

- Superficial pouch contains external genitalia & associated skeletal muscles, including bulbospongiosus, ischiocavernosus, & superficial transverse perineal muscles. Muscles cover erectile tissue.
- Sup. transverse perineal muscle: support pelvic floor, fix perineal body
- Bulbospongiosus: “sphincter” of vagina aids in erection, compresses greater vestibular gland.
- Ishiocavernosus: forces blood from crura to corpora cavernosa, maintains erection of clitoris.

## Slide 16: Arteries of the Perineum

- Inferior rectal a. to the anal canal inferior to the pectinate line, anal sphincter & peri-anal skin
- Perineal a. to superficial perineal muscles
- Posterior labial a. to skin of posterior labia
- A. of bulb of vestibule to bulb of vestibular bulbs and greater vestibular glands
- Deep a of clitoris to crura of clitoris
- Dorsal a of clitoris to glans of clitoris
- Posterior scrotal a to skin of posterior scrotum
- A. of bulb of penis to supply bulb, penile urethra, corpus spongiosum, bulbourethral glands
- deep a of penis - travels through the center of each corpus cavernosum; gives off straight and helicine aa, which open directly into the sinusoids of the corpora.
- dorsal a of penis to corpora
- superficial external pudendal a to anterior scrotum

## Slide 17: Innervation of Pelvis & Perineum

- Sensory & motor innervation of skin, skeletal muscle
- Somatic division of PNS: sacral plexus (L4-S4)
  - Nerve to obturator internus (L5-S1)
  - Nerve to piriformis (S1-S2)
  - Nerve to levator ani (S4, S5)
  - Nerve to coccygeus (S4, S5 )
  - Pudendal nerve (S2-S4) (perineum)
- Motor innervation of viscera
- Autonomic division of PNS: inferior hypogastric plexus
  - Sympathetic innervation
    - Lumbar & sacral splanchnics: effects – vasoconstriction, contraction of internal urethral (male) & anal sphincters, stimulate glandular secretion, ejaculation (male)
  - Parasympathetic innervation
    - Pelvic splanchnics (S2-S4): effects – vasodilation, contraction of the detrusor, engorgement of erectile tissue.

## Slide 18: Autonomic Regulation of Sexual Function

- Erection: parasympathetic (S2-4 via pelvic splanchnic nn)
- Emission: sympathetic (L1-2)
- Smooth muscle contraction of the ductus deferens to move sperm into the urethra. Contractions of seminal vesicles, prostate, and bulbourethral glands secrete seminal fluid into the urethra.
- Ejaculation:
- Sympathetic (L1-2): rhythmic contraction of smooth muscle in the ductus deferens & urethra; closure of internal urethral sphincter. Somatic (S2-4, pudendal n): contraction of bulbospongiosus & ischiocavernosus muscles compress the bases of the penile erectile tissue.
- Point = Parasympathetic = Erection
- Shoot = Sympathetic = Ejaculation

## Slide 19: Pudendal Nerve Block

- Pudendal nerve:
- Passes out of the pelvis via the greater sciatic foramen & around the sacrospinous ligament into the lesser sciatic foramen, where it enters the pudendal (Alcock's) canal; provides somatic innervation (S2-S4) to the skin & skeletal muscles of the perineum & gives rise to inferior rectal (anal), perineal, labial, & dorsal clitoral nerve branches.
- To relieve perineal pain during childbirth, pudendal nerve block anesthesia may be performed by injecting a local anesthetic agent into the tissues surrounding the pudendal nerve.
- To abolish sensation from the anterior part of the perineum, an ilioinguinal nerve block is performed.

## Slide 20: Male Urethral Rupture

- Straddle injury: rupture of the spongy urethra below the perineal membrane. Urine extravasates to the scrotum, penis, superficial perineum, and lower anterior abdominal wall.
- Pelvic fractures: rupture of the membranous (intermediate part) of the urethra. Urine extravasates to the deep perineal pouch.
