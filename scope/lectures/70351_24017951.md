Early Pregnancy Quick Guide M2 TBL
For every patient presenting in early pregnancy:
Is she pregnant?
- Positive urine pregnancy test o serum β-hCG
How pregnant is she?
- Determine gestational age using the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP) +/- ultrasound when dates are uncertain
Where is the pregnancy?
- Confirm with ultrasound and if unable to confirm, consider a possible ectopic pregnancy.
Is the pregnancy viable?
- Assess symptoms, trend serum β-hCG levels and use ultrasound to confirm
Does she need urgent evaluation?
Look for:
- Severe abdominal pain
- Heavy vaginal bleeding
- Syncope
- Shoulder pain
- Hemodynamic instability
What can be done to improve outcomes for both parent and child?
Consider opportunities for:
- Preconception counseling and early prenatal care
- Lifestyle management opportunities (including nutrition, exercise and smoking cessation)
- Folic acid and iron supplementation
- Vaccination
- Social and behavioral support
- Chronic disease optimization (preferably before pregnancy)
Pregnancy Dating
Gestational age is calculated from the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP).
Gestational age ≠ fetal age
Example:
- LMP was 8 weeks ago
- Gestational age = 8 weeks
- Fertilization occurred approximately 6 weeks ago
Dating Matters Because It:
- Determines estimated due date (EDD) and expected ultrasound findings
- Guides prenatal care and testing
Estimated Due Date (Nägele's Rule)
EDD = LMP + 1 year − 3 months + 7 days
β-hCG Physiology
The syncytiotrophoblast produces human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) after implantation.
hCG is important because hCG maintains the corpus luteum, which produces progesterone needed to maintain the endometrium and support the pregnancy.
What Is hCG Used For Clinically?
- Confirms pregnancy and helps determine viability
- Helps evaluate possible ectopic pregnancy
- Guides interpretation of ultrasound findings
Key Concept
Trends are usually more useful than a single value.
In a normal early pregnancy, β-hCG generally rises over time.
Normal Ultrasound Milestones
(Transvaginal Ultrasound)

| Gestational Age | Expected Finding |
| --- | --- |
| 5 weeks | Gestational sac |
| 5-6 weeks | Yolk sac |
| 6 weeks | Fetal pole |
| 6-7 weeks | Cardiac activity |

Easy Way to Remember: Sac → Yolk Sac → Fetal Pole → Heartbeat
If expected findings are absent, consider:
- Incorrect dating
- Early pregnancy loss
- Ectopic pregnancy
Major Causes of First-Trimester Bleeding
Normal Early Pregnancy: Implantation bleeding can cause mild spotting
Pregnancy Loss: Bleeding with cramping may signal a nonviable pregnancy
Ectopic Pregnancy: Positive pregnancy test, pain ± bleeding, pregnancy outside the uterine cavity
The Diagnosis You Cannot Miss:
Ectopic Pregnancy
In any pregnant patient with pelvic pain + vaginal bleeding + unconfirmed intrauterine pregnancy
Look out for emergency warning signs
- Syncope
- Shoulder pain
- Severe abdominal pain
- Hemodynamic instability
Pregnancy, Lifelong Health and Chronic Disease
Pregnancy is more than a nine-month event. It is a unique opportunity to improve the health of both the pregnant individual and their future child.
Many adverse pregnancy outcomes are influenced by pre-pregnancy and early pregnancy factors, including hypertension or being at risk of developing hypertension/hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, diabetes or being at risk of developing gestational diabetes, obesity, smoking, inadequate nutrition, lack of folic acid supplementation, and delayed prenatal care.
Optimizing health before and during pregnancy may reduce the risk of:
- Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy
- Gestational diabetes
- Preterm birth
- Fetal growth abnormalities
- Some congenital anomalies
Healthy pregnancies are associated with improved lifelong outcomes for both parent and child. Pregnancy may also provide an opportunity to identify and possibly prevent chronic health conditions that affect long-term well-being for both the individual and the entire community.
Key Take-Home Message
Preventing adverse pregnancy outcomes is not only about improving pregnancy and birth outcomes. It is an investment in the lifelong health of the parent, the child, and the community.
